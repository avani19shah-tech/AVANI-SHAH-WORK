BRIEF HISTORY
Index patient AB, 14-year-old Female, Muslim, resides in Khumbhraj, studied till Grade 4 from a public school in Khumbhraj, from a low socio-economic strata was brought for psychological evaluation by her mother, who served as the primary informant. The informants were considered reliable, and the information obtained was largely consistent.

According to the mother, the chief concerns are seizures present since approximately 4 years of age (past 10 years), extreme anger outbursts with verbal aggression including use of abusive language, and frequent physical fights with siblings and peers. Currently, she is not on any anti-epileptic medication. There is a positive family history of seizures, as her father also had seizures. The patient also has a congenital muscular deformity in the right hand since birth, due to which she is unable to lift her right arm or put pressure on it, significantly limiting her ability to perform various tasks independently.

The patient resides in a joint family with a large number of cousins and extended family members. The primary caregiver is her mother, and parents are described as caring and supportive. However, the home environment is reported to be one where the child is frequently bullied by others, which reportedly triggers her anger outbursts. Child-rearing practices at home include physical punishment.

The patient previously attended school but exhibited poor adjustment, low attendance, and poor academic performance. She frequently got into fights at school. She displays extreme anger outbursts, particularly when anyone attempts to converse with or confront her. Adequate attention and concentration were reported, and her general activity level was described as normal; however, significant aggression was noted. She is adequately sociable, has low motivation to engage in work, and can easily adapt to new environments.

The birth took place at home (home delivery) with some complications during the perinatal period. Due to difficulties during delivery, the patient sustained an injury resulting in a muscular deformity of the right hand, which has been present since birth. She cannot lift her right hand or exert pressure on it. Birth cry was present, and postnatal development was reported to be adequate. General developmental milestones were achieved within expected timeframes, except for the motor limitation in the right hand secondary to the birth injury. No other significant physical illness was reported. The family has poor medical compliance.

OVERALL IMPRESSION 
AB is a 14-year-old female from a low socio-economic background, educated till Grade 4, who presents with a history of seizures since approximately 4 years of age (currently unmedicated), extreme anger outbursts with verbal aggression and abusive language, frequent physical fights with siblings and peers, and a congenital muscular deformity of the right hand since birth. She has a positive family history of seizures (father). She previously attended school but had poor adjustment, low attendance, poor academic performance, and frequent altercations. She resides in a joint family where she is reportedly bullied, contributing to her aggressive behaviour. Child-rearing practices include punishment. During evaluation, she was largely cooperative but displayed intermittent aggression and low sustained motivation. Eye contact was maintained, attention and concentration were adequate, and temper tantrums were observed during the assessment. On the Vineland Social Maturity Scale, her social age was 5 years 9 months with a social quotient of 39, indicating moderate disability in social and adaptive functioning. Severe deficits were noted across most domains including self-help general, self-help dressing, self-direction (not attained), occupation, communication, locomotion, and socialization. On the Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children (Malin's Indian Adaptation), her Full Scale IQ was 47, falling within the Moderate Intellectual Disability range. Verbal IQ (62) indicated Mild Intellectual Disability, while Performance IQ (33) indicated Severe Intellectual Disability, with the significant discrepancy likely influenced by the congenital muscular deformity of the right hand. Overall, the findings indicate Moderate Intellectual Disability with significant deficits in adaptive functioning, exacerbated by a congenital physical deformity, uncontrolled seizure disorder, and behavioural difficulties including severe anger outbursts and aggression.


RECOMMENDATION AND SUGGESTIONS 

Neurological consultation and medication compliance: Referral to a neurologist for appropriate anti-epileptic medication management, given the 10-year history of seizures and current non-medicated status. Psychoeducation regarding the importance of medication compliance.
Anger management and emotional regulation training: Structured interventions to help her identify triggers of anger, recognise emotions, develop coping strategies such as deep breathing and time-out techniques, and reduce the frequency and intensity of aggressive outbursts.
Parent and caregiver training: To understand the child's difficulties, manage anger outbursts, replace punishment-oriented child-rearing practices with positive parenting techniques, and reduce bullying within the home environment.
Behaviour modification programme: Implementation of a systematic plan using positive reinforcement to increase adaptive behaviours and reduce maladaptive behaviours such as physical aggression, verbal abuse, and temper tantrums.
Social skills training: To improve interpersonal interactions, turn-taking, appropriate expression of needs, conflict resolution, and age-appropriate social behaviour with peers.
Adaptive and daily living skills training: Structured training in self-care activities, self-help skills, and daily living tasks appropriate to her functional level, with accommodations for the right-hand deformity.
Occupational therapy: Referral for occupational therapy to maximize functional use of the left hand as the dominant hand and develop compensatory strategies for the right-hand deformity.
Special education and vocational training: Enrolment in a special education programme suited to her cognitive level, with focus on functional academics and pre-vocational skills training that accommodate her physical limitations.
Speech and communication training: Interventions to enhance expressive and receptive communication skills, given the severe disability noted in the communication domain.
Follow-up and periodic reassessment: Regular follow-up to monitor progress, medication compliance, seizure control, and behavioural changes.
