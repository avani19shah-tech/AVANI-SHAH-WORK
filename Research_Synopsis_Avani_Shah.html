Research Synopsis

Title: Effect of Emotion-Focused Skills Training on Alexithymia in Opioid-Dependent Individuals With and Without HIV: A Pilot Pretest-Posttest Study Using Psychometric and Projective Assessment
Researcher: Avani Shah
Programme: MPhil Clinical Psychology
Guide / Supervisor: Dr. Vasudha Singh
Institution: Man College of Special Education and Psychological Studies, Guna

1. Introduction and Background

Opioid dependence is one of the most severe substance use disorders worldwide, characterised by compulsive drug-seeking behaviour, physiological tolerance, and progressive psychosocial deterioration. In India, opioid use disorder has risen sharply, with injection drug use constituting a primary vector for HIV transmission. Approximately 22-28% of people who inject drugs (PWID) in India are living with HIV (NACO, 2022), creating a dual-burden population with compounded psychological vulnerabilities.

A critical yet under-investigated psychological dimension in this population is alexithymia — a personality construct characterised by difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing feelings to others, and externally oriented thinking. The construct was introduced by Sifneos (1973) and has since been measured reliably using the Toronto Alexithymia Scale-20 (TAS-20; Bagby, Parker & Taylor, 1994), which demonstrates robust psychometric properties across cultures.

Research consistently demonstrates elevated alexithymia prevalence (40-67%) in opioid-dependent individuals compared to the general population (8-10%). Alexithymia in addiction serves as both a predisposing vulnerability and a maintaining mechanism — individuals with limited emotional awareness use substances to regulate undifferentiated negative affect states (Khantzian, 1997). Concurrently, emerging evidence links HIV-related neuropathological changes to worsened emotional processing, with alexithymia predicting poorer medication adherence and greater disease severity in HIV-positive populations (Berger et al., 2015; Tull et al., 2013).

Despite the independent association of alexithymia with both opioid dependence and HIV, no study has examined whether co-occurring HIV in opioid-dependent individuals intensifies alexithymia, nor whether a targeted emotion-focused intervention can differentially reduce alexithymia in these subgroups. Furthermore, assessment has predominantly relied on self-report alone, neglecting projective methods (Rorschach, TAT) that capture implicit emotional processing deficits.

2. Need for the Study / Research Gap

  1. No comparative study of alexithymia between HIV-positive and HIV-negative opioid-dependent individuals exists in the literature.
  2. No intervention study has targeted alexithymia specifically in opioid-dependent individuals stratified by HIV status.
  3. The integration of projective assessment (Rorschach, TAT) with psychometric measures (TAS-20) to assess alexithymia in this population has never been attempted.
  4. No Indian study has examined alexithymia as an intervention target in de-addiction rehabilitation settings.
  5. The unique opportunity of a structured 1-month inpatient de-addiction programme to deliver and evaluate emotion-focused interventions remains unutilised.
  6. No pilot/feasibility data exists for emotion-focused skills training within the timeframe of standard inpatient de-addiction treatment.

3. Aim of the Study

To evaluate the effectiveness of a structured Emotion-Focused Skills Training (EFST) intervention in reducing alexithymia among opioid-dependent inpatients, and to examine whether the presence of HIV comorbidity moderates treatment response, using an integrated psychometric and projective assessment approach.

4. Objectives

  1. To assess and compare baseline levels of alexithymia (TAS-20 total and subscale scores) between HIV-positive and HIV-negative opioid-dependent inpatients in a de-addiction facility.
  2. To evaluate the pre-post change in alexithymia following an 8-session Emotion-Focused Skills Training intervention in both groups.
  3. To examine whether HIV status moderates the effectiveness of EFST in reducing alexithymia (Group × Time interaction effect).
  4. To assess alexithymia through projective assessment methods (Rorschach affect-related variables and TAT emotional narrative quality) and examine convergence with self-report measures.
  5. To explore the relationship between alexithymia, emotion regulation difficulties (DERS), and depression (BDI-II) in opioid-dependent individuals with and without HIV.
  6. To establish pilot feasibility data (retention rates, session attendance, participant feedback) for EFST in an inpatient de-addiction context.

5. Hypotheses

Primary Hypotheses:

  1. H1: HIV-positive opioid-dependent inpatients will demonstrate significantly higher baseline alexithymia (TAS-20) scores compared to HIV-negative opioid-dependent inpatients.
  2. H2: There will be a significant reduction in alexithymia (TAS-20 total and subscale scores) from pre-test to post-test following EFST in both groups.
  3. H3: The magnitude of reduction in alexithymia following EFST will differ significantly between HIV-positive and HIV-negative groups (significant Group × Time interaction).

Secondary Hypotheses:

  1. H4: TAS-20 scores will show significant correlations with Rorschach affect constriction indicators (low Afr, low FC, low Blends).
  2. H5: Higher alexithymia (TAS-20) will be significantly associated with greater emotion regulation difficulties (DERS total score) in both groups.
  3. H6: Projective assessment indicators of emotional processing (Rorschach, TAT) will show significant improvement from pre-test to post-test following EFST.

9. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

9.1 Inclusion Criteria:

  1. Diagnosis of Opioid Dependence Syndrome as per ICD-11 (6C43) / DSM-5 Opioid Use Disorder (Moderate to Severe)
  2. Male gender
  3. Age range: 18-50 years
  4. Inpatient in de-addiction facility for minimum 1 month
  5. Minimum 1 week of detoxification completed (post-acute withdrawal phase)
  6. For HIV+ group: Documented HIV-positive status (ELISA confirmed)
  7. For HIV- group: Documented HIV-negative status (tested within last 3 months)
  8. Ability to read and understand the language of assessment
  9. Informed written consent to participate

9.2 Exclusion Criteria:

  1. Comorbid psychotic disorders (Schizophrenia, Bipolar I with psychotic features)
  2. Severe cognitive impairment or intellectual disability (IQ < 70)
  3. Active neurological disorder (epilepsy, traumatic brain injury, stroke)
  4. HIV-associated dementia or severe neurocognitive disorder (HAD Stage 3-4)
  5. Primary dependence on substances other than opioids
  6. Acute medical instability requiring intensive care
  7. Previous formal training in emotion regulation or mindfulness-based interventions (within last 6 months)
  8. Refusal to consent or inability to commit to 4-week inpatient stay

12. Procedure

PhaseTimelineActivities
Phase 1: Screening & Enrollment Week 1 (Days 7-10) Screen patients; Obtain informed consent; Confirm HIV status from records; Assign groups; Collect sociodemographic data
Phase 2: Pre-Test Assessment Week 1 (Days 8-12) Administer TAS-20, DERS, BDI-II, SDS, Rorschach (45-60 min), TAT (30-45 min)
Phase 3: Intervention Weeks 2-3 Deliver 8 EFST sessions (both groups); Maintain attendance records; Session feedback
Phase 4: Post-Test Assessment Week 4 (Days 25-28) Re-administer: TAS-20, DERS, BDI-II, Rorschach, TAT; Participant feedback
Phase 5: Data Analysis Post-collection Scoring; Inter-rater reliability; Statistical analyses; Interpretation

14. Ethical Considerations

  1. Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) approval will be obtained prior to data collection.
  2. Written informed consent will be obtained in the participant's preferred language.
  3. Strict confidentiality of HIV status will be maintained throughout the study.
  4. Participants will have the right to withdraw at any time without impact on their ongoing treatment.
  5. No deception is involved in any aspect of the study.
  6. Participants experiencing distress will be immediately referred to the treating psychiatrist/psychologist.
  7. All data will be stored securely with coded identifiers (de-identified).
  8. All participants will continue to receive standard de-addiction treatment regardless of study participation.
  9. The study will be registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) if required by the institution.

15. Expected Outcomes

  1. HIV-positive opioid-dependent individuals are expected to demonstrate significantly higher baseline alexithymia compared to HIV-negative counterparts.
  2. Both groups are expected to show statistically significant reduction in TAS-20 scores following the 8-session EFST intervention.
  3. The HIV-negative group may show greater magnitude of improvement due to fewer neurobiological constraints on emotional processing recovery.
  4. Rorschach affect variables and TAT emotional narrative quality are expected to show positive change correlating with TAS-20 improvement.
  5. High retention (≥80%) and session attendance (≥75%) are anticipated given the controlled inpatient environment.

16. Significance of the Study

Clinical Significance:

  • Demonstrates that EFST can be incorporated into standard 1-month de-addiction programmes.
  • Enables treatment personalisation based on HIV status if differential responses are found.
  • Improved emotional processing may contribute to relapse prevention and better medication adherence.
  • Establishes clinical utility of projective assessment (Rorschach, TAT) in addiction treatment settings.

Academic Significance:

  • First study to compare alexithymia between HIV+ and HIV- opioid-dependent individuals.
  • First intervention study targeting alexithymia in opioid-HIV comorbidity.
  • First multi-method (psychometric + projective) alexithymia assessment in this population.
  • Provides pilot data for designing a larger randomised controlled trial.
  • Contributes to the alexithymia trait-vs-state debate in addiction contexts.

Contextual Significance:

  • Addresses a neglected psychological dimension of India's opioid-HIV epidemic.
  • Provides a low-cost, implementable intervention model for resource-limited de-addiction settings.
  • Generates Indian normative data on alexithymia in this clinical population.

17. Limitations

  1. Absence of a true no-intervention control group (ethical constraint in inpatient settings).
  2. Small sample size (N=60) appropriate for a pilot but limiting generalisability.
  3. Short intervention duration (8 sessions / 4 weeks) for a partially trait-based construct.
  4. No post-discharge follow-up to assess sustainability of gains.
  5. Medication confounds (OST, ART, psychotropics) that may independently affect emotional processing.
  6. Male-only sample limits generalisability to female populations.
  7. Purposive sampling from a single centre limits external validity.
  8. Potential practice effects with Rorschach/TAT re-administration within 4 weeks.
