Case Formulation Diagram

Inside Factors
(Things Within the Person)
Body & Brain Factors (Born with + Developed later):
• Father had alcohol habit and died of cancer
• Brother also overthinks like her
• Possible family tendency to worry and think too much
• Born through C-section (no complications)
Thinking Patterns & Habits (Developed over time):
• Tendency to overthink everything
• Being overly suspicious of others
• Childhood temper tantrums and stubborn nature
• Low ability to handle stress (gets irritated quickly)
• Reacts without thinking when upset
Outside Factors
(Things Around the Person)
Life Stresses & Surroundings:
• Strained home atmosphere during childhood (frequent family fights)
• Occasional fights with husband (1-2 times/month)
• Problems related to neighbors
• Issues with husband's brother's family
• Managing multiple roles (beautician + trainer + homemaker)
↓
↓
MIDDLE PATHWAY (Where Inside + Outside Meet)
Difficulty Calming Down:
• Gets easily worked up emotionally
• Hard to calm down once upset
• Impulsive reactions to stress
• Irritability builds quickly
↔
Faulty Thinking Patterns:
• Jumps to conclusions about others' intentions
• Sees threat where there may be none
• Assumes the worst about neighbors/in-laws
• Overthinks situations
↓
Main Problem Experiences
Suspicious Thinking & Overthinking
• Being overly suspicious of others (neighbors, in-laws)
• Excessive thinking that won't stop
• Possibly believing others are against her or talking about her
• Difficulty trusting people around her
↓
How She Makes Sense of Her Experiences:

First Reaction (What does this mean?):
"These people really are against me"
"My suspicions are correct — I can see it"

Second Reaction (Can I handle this?):
"I can't stop thinking about it"
"I need to keep my guard up"
"This is getting too much for me" (decreased interest in housework)
↓
Short-Term Effects (What Happens Right Away)
  • Feelings: Irritability, frustration, anxiety, distrust
  • Actions: Arguments with husband about neighbors/in-laws, possibly avoiding certain people, being watchful
  • Thoughts: Keeps replaying situations in mind, focuses only on "evidence" that supports suspicions
  • Ways of Coping: Stays busy with work, talks to husband, but may also withdraw from housework
↓
Long-Term Effects (What Happens Over Time)
  • Pulling away from people: May avoid neighbors, reduce social contact, less interest in kitty parties
  • Fewer chances to correct wrong beliefs: Less interaction means fewer chances to see that people may not actually be against her
  • Daily life affected: Decreased interest in housework (past 2 months), may affect work motivation over time
  • How others see her: Family sees her as "someone who thinks too much" — may feel misunderstood
↑
Left Loop — Back to Inside Factors:

• Biased thinking becomes a habit (always looking for "proof" that others are against her)
• Behaviour matches the biased thinking (avoids people, stays guarded)

↑

• Emotional tension keeps building up over time
• Becomes more easily upset and irritated
(Feeds back into her thinking patterns and emotional state ↑)
↑
Right Loop — Back to Outside Factors:

• Only notices information that fits her suspicions (ignores friendly gestures, remembers slights)
• This feeds back into how she judges new situations

↑

• Pulling away from people actually increases stress (more isolated, less support)
• Conflicts may increase because of her suspicious behaviour
(Feeds back into life stresses and surroundings ↑)
How to Read This Diagram:

This diagram shows how the patient's problems keep going in a cycle rather than getting better on their own:

1. Inside factors (family tendency to overthink, her own suspicious thinking style, low stress tolerance) combine with outside factors (childhood conflicts, neighbor problems, multiple responsibilities).

2. These come together in the middle pathway where she has trouble calming down AND starts thinking in biased ways (assuming the worst).

3. This produces the main problems — excessive suspicion and overthinking.

4. She then interprets these experiences as real ("people really are against me") and feels she can't control the thinking.

5. This leads to short-term effects (irritability, arguments, selective attention to "threats") and then long-term effects (pulling away from people, fewer chances to correct wrong beliefs, less interest in daily tasks).

6. The two feedback loops on the sides show how the long-term effects feed back into both her inside vulnerabilities (making her thinking more biased) and outside stresses (increasing isolation and conflict) — keeping the whole cycle going.
