Background: Psychosis-like experiences (PLEs) are subclinical psychotic phenomena that may represent early manifestations of psychosis and are increasingly recognized within neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs). Although PLEs have been investigated in conditions such as autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), the prevalence, clinical characteristics, associated risk factors, and implications for later psychosis have not been comprehensively synthesized across neurodevelopmental disorders.

Method: A systematic review will be conducted in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Electronic databases including PubMed, PsycINFO, Scopus, and Web of Science will be searched for observational studies investigating psychosis-like experiences among individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders. Data regarding prevalence, phenomenology, assessment methods, associated risk factors, and longitudinal outcomes will be extracted. Study quality will be assessed using standardized risk-of-bias tools. Findings will be synthesized narratively, with meta-analysis performed where appropriate.

Results: The review will synthesize current evidence regarding the prevalence and clinical presentation of psychosis-like experiences across neurodevelopmental disorders, identify shared biological, cognitive, and environmental risk factors, examine their potential role as markers of later psychotic disorders, and evaluate current approaches to differential diagnosis and clinical assessment.

Conclusion: This review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of existing evidence to inform early identification, differential diagnosis, and clinical management of psychosis-like experiences in neurodevelopmental disorders, while highlighting key gaps for future research.

Psychosis-Like Experiences in Individuals with Neurodevelopmental Disorders: A Systematic Review of Prevalence, Phenomenology, Risk Factors, and Clinical Implications
Avani Shah1*, Samriddhi Oli1, Dr. Ajay Sharma1
1Department of Clinical Psychology, Man College of Special Education and Psychological Studies, Guna, Madhya Pradesh, India
*Corresponding Author: Avani Shah | Email: [your email address]

Psychosis-like experiences (PLEs) are subclinical psychotic phenomena—including attenuated hallucinations, paranoid ideation, and unusual perceptual experiences—that exist on a continuum with clinical psychosis and are increasingly recognized within neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs). Although PLEs have been investigated individually in conditions such as autism spectrum disorder (ASD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and intellectual disability (ID), no comprehensive review has synthesized evidence across the full spectrum of neurodevelopmental conditions. The present systematic review, conducted in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines, will search PubMed, PsycINFO, Scopus, and Web of Science for observational studies investigating PLEs among individuals with NDDs. Data regarding prevalence rates, phenomenological profiles, assessment methods, associated risk factors (biological, cognitive, and environmental), and longitudinal outcomes will be extracted. Study quality will be assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and Joanna Briggs Institute checklists. Findings will be synthesized narratively, with meta-analysis where data permit. The review will synthesize evidence regarding elevated prevalence of PLEs across NDDs, identify shared mechanisms (including genetic architecture, dopaminergic dysregulation, and environmental adversity), examine the predictive value of PLEs for psychotic disorder onset, and evaluate approaches to differential diagnosis. This comprehensive synthesis aims to inform early identification, differential diagnosis, and clinical management of PLEs in NDDs, while identifying critical gaps for future longitudinal and intervention research.

Keywords: psychosis-like experiences; neurodevelopmental disorders; autism spectrum disorder; ADHD; intellectual disability; schizophrenia; psychosis continuum; PRISMA
