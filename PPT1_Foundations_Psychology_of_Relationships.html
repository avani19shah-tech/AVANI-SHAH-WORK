Section 1

Why Couple Therapy? The Evolution of the Field

10 Minutes

Why Couple Therapy Matters

The Problem

  • Relationship distress → depression, anxiety, physical illness
  • Divorce rates rising in India (1.1% → trending upward)
  • Children of conflicted marriages show poorer outcomes
  • DV affects 30% of Indian married women (NFHS-5)

The Solution

  • Couple therapy is effective (EFT: 70-73% recovery rate)
  • Can treat individual disorders through relational work
  • Prevention possible through premarital counselling
  • Growing demand in India – few trained professionals
Key Point: You are training for one of the most needed and least available specialties in Indian mental health.

Historical Evolution of Couple Therapy

💬 CLASS DISCUSSION – 3 Minutes

Indian Marriage: What Makes It Different?

"How is marriage in India fundamentally different from the Western model that most textbooks describe?"

Think about:

  • Who decides? (Family vs. individual choice)
  • Who lives together? (Joint family vs. nuclear)
  • What is the purpose? (Individual happiness vs. family alliance/dharma)
  • What is the exit option? (Stigma of divorce)

Indian vs. Western Concepts of Marriage

Western Model

  • Individual choice (love-based)
  • Nuclear family unit
  • Egalitarian partnership ideal
  • Emotional fulfillment as purpose
  • Divorce as valid option
  • Therapist works with the couple alone

Indian Model

  • Family involvement (90% arranged)
  • Extended/joint family system
  • Hierarchical (age, gender roles)
  • Dharma, duty, family honour
  • Divorce = social failure/stigma
  • In-laws, community are stakeholders
Clinical Implication: Western therapy models must be culturally adapted. The "couple" in Indian therapy is often embedded in a multi-generational system. (Jhamb et al., 2024)

Section 2

Relationship Science: What Predicts Success & Failure?

10 Minutes
📊 QUICK POLL

What do you think is the #1 predictor of divorce?

A. Frequent arguments
B. Lack of sex
C. Contempt
D. Financial stress
Answer: C – Contempt! Gottman's research shows contempt (disrespect, mockery, superiority) is the single strongest predictor of divorce. He can predict divorce with 90%+ accuracy from just minutes of observation.

Gottman's Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse

Four destructive communication patterns that predict relationship failure:

HorsemanWhat It Looks LikeAntidote
🗡️ Criticism"You ALWAYS..." / "You NEVER..." (character attack)Gentle Startup: "I feel... about... I need..."
😤 ContemptEye-rolling, mockery, sarcasm, name-callingBuild culture of appreciation
🛡️ Defensiveness"It's not my fault!" / counter-attack / playing victimAccept responsibility (even 2%)
🧱 StonewallingShutting down, walking away, silent treatmentSelf-soothe → return to conversation
The Magic Ratio: Stable couples have 5:1 ratio of positive to negative interactions. Below 5:1 = relationship in danger.

What Makes Relationships Succeed? (Evidence-Based)

Gottman's Sound Relationship House

  1. Love Maps – Knowing partner's inner world
  2. Fondness & Admiration – Expressing appreciation
  3. Turning Toward – Responding to emotional bids
  4. Positive Perspective – Giving benefit of doubt
  5. Manage Conflict – Not resolve ALL conflict (69% is perpetual!)
  6. Make Life Dreams Come True – Support partner's aspirations
  7. Shared Meaning – Rituals, goals, values together

Walls: Trust + Commitment hold everything together

Research: Built on 40+ years of longitudinal research with 3,000+ couples (Gottman & Gottman, 2015)

Section 3

Attachment Theory in Adult Romantic Relationships

10 Minutes

Attachment Theory: From Cradle to Couple

Bowlby's Core Ideas (1969)

  • Humans have innate need for close bonds
  • Early caregiver experiences → Internal Working Models
  • Models guide adult relationship behaviour
  • Attachment system activated by threat/stress

Hazan & Shaver (1987)

  • Applied attachment theory to adult romance
  • Same system that bonds infant-caregiver bonds adult partners
  • Partner serves as "safe haven" + "secure base"
  • Three (later four) adult attachment styles
Clinical Insight: Attachment theory is the foundation of EFT – the most research-validated couple therapy approach (70-73% success rate; Wiebe et al., 2024)

Adult Attachment Styles

StyleView of SelfView of OtherIn Relationship
Secure (~55%)Worthy of loveReliable, responsiveComfortable with closeness & independence
Anxious (~20%)UnworthyDesired but unreliablePursuer: clingy, jealous, hypervigilant
Avoidant (~20%)Self-sufficientUnreliableWithdrawer: distances, suppresses emotion
Disorganized (~5%)UnworthyDangerousDesires closeness but fears it; chaotic
The Most Common Distressed Pattern: Anxious-Avoidant pairing → Pursuer-Withdrawer cycle. She pursues with criticism → He withdraws → She pursues harder → He shuts down further. THIS IS THE CYCLE WE TREAT IN EFT.
✋ PAIR ACTIVITY – 5 Minutes

Identify the Attachment Style

In pairs, identify the likely attachment style for each vignette:

Vignette 1

"When my husband works late, I keep calling him. If he doesn't pick up, I imagine the worst. I need constant reassurance that he loves me."

→ Anxious-Preoccupied

Vignette 2

"I don't see why my wife needs to talk about feelings all the time. I show love by providing. When she gets emotional, I feel like leaving the room."

→ Dismissive-Avoidant

Vignette 3 (Indian context)

"After marriage, I moved to his joint family. His mother controls everything. When I try to talk to him, he says 'Don't create drama.' I feel invisible."

→ Wife: Anxious pursuit (attachment need unmet). Husband: Avoidant withdrawal + triangulation with mother. Classic pursuer-withdrawer in Indian joint family context.

Section 4

Love, Personality & Emotion in Marriage

10 Minutes

Sternberg's Triangular Theory of Love (1986)

Three Components

  • Intimacy – Emotional closeness, warmth, sharing
  • Passion – Physical attraction, sexual desire, arousal
  • Commitment – Decision to love + maintain relationship

Consummate Love = All three present (the ideal)

Clinical Application 🇮🇳

  • Arranged marriage: Often starts with Commitment only → builds Intimacy → Passion may/may not develop
  • Love marriage: Often starts with Passion → needs to build Commitment + deeper Intimacy
  • Empty Love: Commitment only – common in long-term distressed Indian marriages
Ask couples: "Which component is strongest? Which needs attention?" – Instant formulation tool.

Personality Factors in Marriage

Big Five & Relationship Outcomes

TraitImpact on Marriage
Neuroticism ⚠️STRONGEST predictor of marital dissatisfaction (Karney & Bradbury, 1995)
AgreeablenessBetter conflict resolution, more accommodation
ConscientiousnessReliability, follow-through, commitment maintenance
ExtraversionSocial engagement but also potential for conflict
OpennessGrowth together vs. divergence over time

Personality Disorders in Marriage (High Difficulty Cases)

  • Narcissistic: Lack of empathy, entitlement, exploitation
  • Borderline: Fear of abandonment, idealization-devaluation
  • OCPD: Rigidity, control, emotional constriction
  • Dependent: Over-reliance, unable to function independently

Emotion Regulation in Couples

Why It Matters

  • Gottman's finding: When heart rate exceeds 100 BPM during conflict → constructive problem-solving becomes IMPOSSIBLE (Diffuse Physiological Arousal)
  • Flooded partners can't listen, empathize, or problem-solve
  • Co-regulation: Partners either calm each other (healthy) or escalate each other (unhealthy)

Clinical Implication

Teach couples: "When you feel flooded, STOP. Take a 20-minute break. Do something self-soothing (walk, breathe, music). Return when calm. This is NOT stonewalling – it's strategic self-regulation."

Emotion Theory Key Concepts

  • Primary emotions: Fear, sadness, vulnerability (often hidden)
  • Secondary emotions: Anger, contempt, indifference (what we show)
  • EFT goal: Access the primary emotions underneath the secondary reactions

🧠 CASE DISCUSSION – 5 Minutes

Quick Case Formulation Practice

Case: Priya (27, software engineer) and Rahul (31, business family), arranged marriage, 2 years. Living with Rahul's parents (joint family). Priya reports: "He never takes my side. His mother decides everything – what I cook, what I wear, when we go out. When I try to talk to Rahul, he says 'She's my mother, what can I do?' I feel like a servant, not a wife." Rahul reports: "She's always complaining. I'm caught in the middle. If I support her, my mother cries. If I support my mother, Priya threatens to leave."

Discuss with your neighbor (2 min):

  1. What is the negative interaction cycle?
  2. What attachment needs are unmet?
  3. What systemic issue is central? (Think Bowen: triangulation, differentiation)
Cycle: Priya pursues (complaints, ultimatums) → Rahul withdraws (avoids, defers to mother) → Priya escalates.
Attachment: Priya – "Do I matter to you?" (unmet need for prioritization). Rahul – "I can't please anyone" (inadequacy, avoidance).
Systemic: Triangulation (Rahul-Priya-Mother). Low differentiation in Rahul. Diffuse boundary between marital and parental subsystem. Structural goal: Strengthen the couple boundary.

Hour 1: Key Takeaways

1. The Field: Couple therapy evolved from advice-giving → systems thinking → emotion-focused work. Evidence base is strong.
2. Indian Context: Marriage in India = family alliance, not just individual partnership. Joint families, arranged marriages, caste, religion all shape the therapy.
3. Gottman's Research: Contempt is the #1 predictor of divorce. 5:1 ratio. 69% of problems are perpetual. Friendship is the foundation.
4. Attachment: Adult romantic love IS attachment. Pursuer-withdrawer is the most common distress pattern. Understanding attachment = understanding the WHY beneath behaviour.
5. Love & Personality: Sternberg helps map what's missing. Neuroticism is the key personality risk factor. Emotion regulation is the engine of change.
