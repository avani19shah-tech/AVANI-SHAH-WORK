TEST ADMINISTERED AND RATIONALE OF THE TEST
Seguin Form Board Test (SFBT)
The Seguin Form Board Test is a performance-based test of non-verbal intelligence that measures visuospatial perception, psychomotor coordination, speed of performance, and cognitive ability. It is particularly useful for individuals with limited or no verbal abilities and those with no formal education, as it does not require literacy or language-based responses.
Vineland Social Maturity Scale (VSMS)
The Vineland Social Maturity Scale (VSMS) is conducted to assess a patient's social competence and adaptive functioning across various domains, including communication, self-help skills, socialization, and daily living abilities.

RESULTS AND INTERPRETATION 
On the Seguin Form Board Test (SFBT), the patient was administered three trials. Her time for Trial 1 was 4 minutes 21 seconds (261 seconds), for Trial 2 was 4 minutes 29 seconds (269 seconds), and for Trial 3 was 2 minutes 11 seconds (131 seconds). The best time (shortest time) of 131 seconds was used for scoring. According to the SFBT norms, a typically developing child of 7-8 years is expected to complete the form board in approximately 30-60 seconds. The patient's best performance time of 131 seconds at the chronological age of 17 years 9 months falls significantly below expected age-level norms, indicating a performance age of approximately 3-4 years. This suggests significant impairment in non-verbal intelligence, visuospatial perception, psychomotor coordination, and cognitive speed, consistent with moderate to severe intellectual disability. A practice effect was observed across trials, with improvement from Trial 1 to Trial 3, indicating some capacity for learning.


On VSMS, the social age of the patient is 7 years 4 months as opposed to the chronological age of 17 years 9 months. It gives a social quotient (SQ) of 49, indicating moderate disability in social and adaptive functioning.

OVERALL IMPRESSION 
RK is a 17-year-old female with no formal education, from a low socio-economic background, who presents with significantly delayed and limited speech since childhood, with only a few meaningful monosyllabic words such as "maa," "papa," and "bua," and no sentence formation. She has been experiencing epileptic seizures for the past two years. All developmental milestones except speech were achieved at appropriate ages. She has received no formal education and has been cared for by her grandparents since birth. During evaluation, she was cooperative, calm, and able to sit at one place. Attention and concentration were adequate. Speech was extremely limited to a few words and she could understand simple instructions but was unable to express herself verbally. On the Seguin Form Board Test, her best performance time was 131 seconds, indicating a performance age of approximately 3-4 years, which is significantly below her chronological age and suggests moderate to severe impairment in non-verbal intelligence, visuospatial perception, and psychomotor coordination. On the Vineland Social Maturity Scale, her social age was 7 years 4 months with a social quotient of 49, indicating moderate disability in social and adaptive functioning. Severe to profound deficits were noted in self-help general, self-direction (not attained), and communication (profound disability), while mild deficits were observed in self-help dressing and occupation. Self-help eating and locomotion were age-appropriate. Overall, the findings indicate that the patient presents with Moderate Intellectual Disability with marked deficits in speech and communication, adaptive functioning, and self-direction, along with a seizure disorder of two years' duration.

RECOMMENDATION AND SUGGESTIONS 

Neurological consultation and seizure management: Referral to a neurologist for appropriate anti-epileptic medication management and regular monitoring of seizure activity.
Speech and language therapy: Intensive speech therapy to develop functional communication skills, increase vocabulary, improve word formation, and establish alternative or augmentative communication strategies where needed.
Adaptive and daily living skills training: Structured training in self-care activities, self-help skills, and daily living tasks appropriate to her functional level, with focus on improving independence in personal hygiene, grooming, and self-direction.
Special education and vocational training: Enrolment in a special education programme or open schooling suited to her cognitive level, with focus on functional academics, life skills, and pre-vocational skills training to enhance her occupational abilities.
Social skills training: Interventions to improve interpersonal interactions, social communication, appropriate expression of needs, and age-appropriate social behaviour with peers and community members.
Parent and caregiver training: Psychoeducation for grandparents and family members about intellectual disability, seizure management, communication strategies, and ways to support the patient's development at home. Training in creating a language-rich and stimulating home environment.
Follow-up and periodic reassessment: Regular follow-up to monitor progress in speech development, seizure control, adaptive functioning, and overall development.
