Overview: Evidence-Based Couple Therapies

What does "evidence-based" mean in couple therapy? Approaches supported by randomized controlled trials (RCTs), replicated findings, and long-term follow-up data.

The Big Four Empirically Supported Approaches

ApproachFoundersCore Idea in One Sentence
Gottman MethodJohn & Julie GottmanBuild friendship and manage conflict using research-based principles
EFTSue Johnson & Les GreenbergRestructure attachment bonds by accessing primary emotions
CBCTEpstein & BaucomChange distorted cognitions and maladaptive behaviours
IBCTChristensen & JacobsonPromote acceptance of differences before pushing for change
Why This Matters: In India, much "couple therapy" is still advice-giving or problem-solving by untrained counsellors. As MPhil trainees, you will practice ONLY evidence-based approaches with documented efficacy.
Not Evidence-Based: Advice-giving, moralizing, taking sides, prescribing "compromise," couple prayer/religious counselling alone (though can supplement), astrology-based matching post-marriage.

Section 1

Gottman Method Couples Therapy

15 Minutes (5–20 min)

Sound Relationship House: 7 Levels

John & Julie Gottman's empirically-derived model of what makes relationships work:

🏠 THE SOUND RELATIONSHIP HOUSE

7. Shared Meaning – Creating rituals, roles, goals, symbols together
6. Make Life Dreams Come True – Supporting partner's aspirations
5. Manage Conflict – Dialogue about perpetual problems (69% unsolvable!)
4. Positive Perspective – Giving partner benefit of doubt
3. Turning Toward – Responding to emotional bids
2. Fondness & Admiration – Expressing appreciation & respect
1. Love Maps – Knowing partner's inner world (dreams, fears, preferences)

WALLS: Trust + Commitment (hold everything together)

Evidence: Built on 40+ years of longitudinal research with 3,000+ couples at the "Love Lab" (Gottman & Gottman, 2015)

The Four Horsemen & Their Antidotes

Four destructive patterns that predict relationship failure with 90%+ accuracy:

HorsemanWhat It Looks LikeAntidote
🗡️ Criticism"You ALWAYS..." / "You NEVER..." (character attack vs. specific complaint)Gentle Startup: "I feel [emotion] about [situation]. I need [request]."
😤 ContemptEye-rolling, mockery, sarcasm, name-calling, superiorityCulture of Appreciation: Express fondness & admiration daily (5:1 ratio)
🛡️ Defensiveness"It's not my fault!" / Counter-attack / Playing victimAccept Responsibility: "You're right, I did forget. Let me fix it."
🧱 StonewallingShutting down, leaving, silent treatment, glazed eyesSelf-Soothe: Take 20-min break, return when HR < 100 BPM
Clinical Alert: Contempt is the SINGLE strongest predictor of divorce. If present, prioritize building fondness & admiration FIRST before any conflict work.

Gottman Method: Session Protocol

PhaseSessionsFocus
Assessment1–3Conjoint interview + individual sessions + questionnaires (Gottman 66 questions, DAS, Oral History Interview)
Feedback4Share assessment findings; identify SRH strengths & weaknesses; set goals; present treatment plan
Friendship & Intimacy5–8Build Love Maps, Fondness & Admiration, Turning Toward (Levels 1-3)

Research Base: 40 years of research | 90%+ divorce prediction accuracy | Couples maintain gains at 2-year follow-up (Gottman & Gottman, 2015)
🎯 ACTIVITY – Emotional Bids

Turning Toward: Recognizing Emotional Bids

An emotional bid is any attempt by a partner to connect – for attention, affection, humor, or support. Partners can: Turn Toward (engage), Turn Away (ignore), or Turn Against (reject).

Scenario 1: Partner says "The sunset is beautiful tonight" while the other is scrolling their phone.

YES – This IS a bid for connection. The partner is inviting shared attention/experience. Turning toward: Put phone down, look up, say "It really is gorgeous." Research: Couples who divorce turn toward only 33% of the time. Happy couples: 86%.

Scenario 2: Husband sighs loudly after coming home and says "What a day..."

YES – A bid for attention & support. Turning toward: "Tell me about it. What happened?" Turning away: Continue watching TV without responding. Turning against: "You think YOU had a bad day?"

Scenario 3: Wife shows husband a funny meme on her phone.

YES – A bid for humor & shared enjoyment. Turning toward: Laugh together, share one back. Every small bid responded to = deposit in the "emotional bank account."
Clinical Tip: In session, train couples to recognize bids. Assignment: Track bids for one week. Count how many you noticed and responded to.

Section 2

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)

15 Minutes (20–35 min)

EFT: Overview & Foundation

Key Facts

  • Founders: Sue Johnson & Les Greenberg (1988)
  • Theory: Attachment-based (Bowlby)
  • Success Rate: 70–73% recovery from distress (Wiebe et al., 2024 meta-analysis)
  • Duration: 8–20 sessions typically
  • Gold Standard: Most research-validated couple therapy

The Core Idea

Relationship distress = insecure attachment

→ Partners cannot access each other as a safe haven/secure base

→ This triggers negative interaction cycles

→ Cycles block access to attachment needs

→ Both partners feel alone, unloved, inadequate

EFT Goal: Create new bonding events by accessing primary emotions and restructuring the interaction cycle

Evidence: 30+ RCTs | Effective across diverse populations | Gains maintained at 2-year follow-up | Effective for PTSD, depression, attachment injuries (Johnson, 2019)

The Pursuer-Withdrawer Cycle

The most common negative interaction pattern in distressed couples (~80% of cases):

She PURSUES
(criticizes, demands, escalates)
Secondary: Anger
→
He WITHDRAWS
(silence, avoidance, shuts down)
Secondary: Numbness
→
She feels ABANDONED
(pursues harder)
Primary: Fear, loneliness
→
He feels INADEQUATE
(withdraws more)
Primary: Shame, helplessness
The EFT Reframe: "The enemy is NOT your partner – the enemy is the CYCLE. You are both trapped in this dance. She pursues because she cares so much. He withdraws because he feels he can never get it right. Underneath, both are saying: 'Do I matter to you? Are you there for me?'"
Clinical Tip: In Indian context, withdrawer is often the husband (socialized to suppress emotion). Pursuer is often the wife, but may appear as "nagging" to family. Normalize BOTH positions as attachment responses.

9 Steps of EFT Across 3 Stages

StageStepSessionsGoal
Stage 1:
De-escalation		1. Identify the conflict1–7Assessment & alliance
2. Identify the negative cycleName the pursuer-withdrawer dance
3. Access underlying emotionsReach primary attachment emotions
4. Reframe: The cycle is the problemExternalize the enemy
Stage 2:

Key Moment: Steps 5 & 7 are the "change events" – when the withdrawer re-engages and the pursuer softens. These are the transformational moments in EFT.

Key EFT Interventions

InterventionDescriptionExample
ReflectionMirror back the emotion"So when he goes quiet, something inside you panics..."
ValidationNormalize the emotional response"Of course you shut down – you learned early that showing feelings wasn't safe."
Evocative RespondingOpen up unexpressed emotion"What happens inside when she turns away? What does that feel like in your body?"
HeighteningAmplify the key emotion"Can you say that again? 'I am afraid I'm losing you.' Say it directly to her."
Empathic ConjectureGuess at the deeper feeling"I wonder if underneath the anger there's a part that says 'I'm not enough for him'?"
Tracking the CycleName the pattern in real-time"Here it is – right now. You're reaching and he's pulling back. The cycle is happening."
RestructuringFacilitate new interaction"Can you turn to him and tell him what you need right now?"
The RISSSC Principle: Repeat, use Images, make it Simple, Slow it down, use Soft voice, and Stay with the Client's experience.
🎬 CASE VIGNETTE – EFT in Action

EFT Demo: Identifying the Therapist's Move

Case: Meera (30) and Arjun (33), married 4 years. Meera says angrily: "You never talk to me! You come home, eat, watch cricket, and sleep. I don't exist for you!" Arjun looks away, crosses arms, goes silent.

Therapist says: "Arjun, I notice you've gone quiet right now. What's happening inside as you hear Meera say this? ... [pause] ... I'm wondering if part of you is thinking 'Nothing I do is ever enough for her' – is that close?"

Question: What EFT interventions is the therapist using? (Select all that apply)

A. Tracking the cycle
B. Evocative responding
C. Empathic conjecture
D. Heightening
Answer: A, B, and C.
A – Tracking: Therapist notices the withdrawal happening in real-time ("I notice you've gone quiet").
B – Evocative Responding: "What's happening inside?" – inviting the withdrawer to access internal experience.
C – Empathic Conjecture: "I'm wondering if part of you thinks..." – guessing at the primary emotion (inadequacy/shame) beneath the secondary (shutdown).
NOT D – Heightening would be asking him to repeat/amplify the feeling directly to Meera.

Section 3

CBCT & IBCT: Cognitive-Behavioral Approaches

10 Minutes (35–45 min)

Cognitive Behavioral Couple Therapy (CBCT)

Founders: Norman Epstein & Donald Baucom (2002)

Three Domains of Intervention

1. Cognitions

  • Attributions: "He's late because he doesn't care" vs. "Traffic was bad"
  • Expectancies: "She'll never change"
  • Assumptions: "If he loved me, he'd know what I need"
  • Standards: "A good husband should always..."

2. Behaviours

  • Positive behaviour exchange (caring days)
  • Communication skills training (speaker-listener)
  • Problem-solving training (5 steps)

3. Emotions

  • Emotion identification & labelling
  • Emotion regulation strategies
  • Expressing vulnerable emotions safely
  • Reducing emotional reactivity

Session Structure

  • Psychoeducation on cognition-emotion-behaviour link
  • Structured exercises & homework
  • Cognitive restructuring of relationship beliefs
  • Behavioural experiments
Indian Application: CBCT particularly useful for couples with rigid cultural standards ("A wife must..." / "A husband should...") that create unrealistic expectations and chronic disappointment.

Integrative Behavioral Couple Therapy (IBCT)

Founders: Andrew Christensen & Neil Jacobson (1998)

DEEP Analysis Framework

ComponentDescriptionExample
DifferencesIncompatibilities that trigger conflictShe's social, he's introverted – fights about weekends
Emotional SensitivitiesVulnerabilities from history/attachmentHis father was critical → hypersensitive to any complaint
External StressorsLife pressures that exacerbate conflictFinancial stress, in-laws, job loss, new baby
PatternsRepetitive interaction cyclesDemand-withdraw, mutual avoidance, mutual attack

Two Types of Strategies

Acceptance Strategies

  • Empathic Joining: Seeing partner's pain beneath their behaviour
  • Unified Detachment: Viewing the problem as "it" (external to both)
  • Tolerance Building: Reducing reactivity through exposure, self-care, faking positives

Change Strategies

  • Behaviour exchange
  • Communication training
  • Problem-solving skills
  • (Same as traditional BCT but used AFTER acceptance)
Evidence: Largest RCT in couple therapy (134 couples, 5-year follow-up). 69% clinically improved at termination. Adopted by U.S. Veterans Affairs since 2010 (Christensen et al., 2016).

Integration Principle: In practice, many therapists integrate elements from multiple approaches. The best fit depends on the couple's presenting issues, readiness for emotional work, and cultural context.

Section 4

Role-Play Practice

10 Minutes (45–55 min)
🎭 EXPERIENTIAL ACTIVITY – 10 Minutes

Role-Play: Applying an Evidence-Based Approach

Setup (1 minute)

  • Form groups of 3 people: Therapist + Partner A + Partner B
  • The Therapist chooses ONE approach to use (Gottman, EFT, CBCT, or IBCT)
  • Partners act out the scenario on the next slide

Process (5 minutes)

  1. Partners begin the conflict scenario
  2. Therapist intervenes using their chosen approach
  3. Focus on 1–2 key interventions (don't try everything!)

Debrief (4 minutes)

  • What approach did the therapist use? What specific intervention?
  • How did it feel as the client? Did you feel heard/understood?
  • What was hardest for the therapist?
Tip for Therapists: You don't need to "fix" anything in 5 minutes. Just demonstrate ONE move – track the cycle, validate an emotion, identify a cognition, or reframe a difference as DEEP.
📋 ROLE-PLAY SCENARIO

Case: Neha & Vikram – In-Law Conflict + Emotional Distance

Context: Neha (28, teacher) & Vikram (32, IT manager), arranged marriage, 3 years. Living in nuclear setup but mother-in-law visits every weekend and stays 2-3 days. Neha feels controlled and invisible. Vikram feels torn.

Neha (Partner A): "Every weekend your mother comes and takes over MY kitchen, MY house. She tells me I'm not cooking right, not keeping the house clean enough. And you just sit there! Last week she told me I should 'learn from her' how to be a good wife. I can't take this anymore!"

Vikram (Partner B): "Here we go again. My mother is old and alone. She just wants to help. You take everything as an insult. I can't tell her to stop coming – she's my MOTHER. What do you want me to do, choose between you?"

Therapist Prompts (choose your approach):

  • If EFT: What is Neha's primary emotion beneath the anger? What is Vikram afraid of? Track the cycle.
  • If Gottman: Identify the Four Horsemen. Help with gentle startup. What bid is Neha making?
  • If CBCT: What cognitive distortions are present? What standards are in conflict?
  • If IBCT: Do a DEEP analysis. How might unified detachment help here?

Hour 3: Key Takeaways

1. Gottman Method: Built on 40 years of research. Sound Relationship House = friendship + conflict management + shared meaning. Four Horsemen predict divorce. Focus on turning toward bids and building a culture of appreciation.
2. EFT: The gold standard. Attachment-based. 70-73% recovery. Focus on accessing primary emotions underneath the negative cycle. The cycle is the enemy, not the partner. Key moments: withdrawer re-engagement + pursuer softening.
3. CBCT: Targets cognitions (distorted attributions, rigid standards), behaviours (communication, problem-solving), and emotions. Structured, skill-building approach.
4. IBCT: Acceptance before change. DEEP analysis for formulation. Empathic joining + unified detachment + tolerance building. Largest RCT evidence base.
5. Integration: No one approach fits all couples. Match the approach to the couple's needs, readiness, and cultural context. In Indian practice, cultural adaptation of all models is essential.
