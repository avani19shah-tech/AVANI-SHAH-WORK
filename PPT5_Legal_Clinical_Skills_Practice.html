Indian Marriage Laws: What Therapists Must Know

⚠️ Remember: You are NOT a legal advisor. Know enough to understand your client's legal context and refer appropriately. Never provide legal opinions.

Grounds for Divorce – Hindu Marriage Act (Section 13)

Grounds Available to Both (S.13(1))

  • Adultery – Voluntary sexual intercourse outside marriage
  • Cruelty – Physical or mental (most common ground filed)
  • Desertion – 2 years continuous
  • Conversion – To another religion
  • Mental disorder – Incurable; intermittent insanity
  • Communicable disease – Venereal, not cured (3 years)
  • Renunciation – Entered religious order
  • Presumption of death – Not heard alive for 7 years

Additional Grounds for Wife (S.13(2))

  • Bigamy (husband married again before 1955 Act)
  • Rape, sodomy, or bestiality by husband
  • No cohabitation resumed after maintenance order (1+ year)
  • Marriage before age 15; repudiated before 18

Mutual Consent Divorce (S.13B)

  • Living separately for 1+ year
  • Both agree marriage cannot be saved
  • 6-month "cooling period" (Supreme Court can waive)
  • Therapists often see couples during this period
💡 Clinical Tip: Many couples in therapy are simultaneously navigating legal proceedings. Understand the timeline so you can align therapeutic work appropriately.

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

Definition of Domestic Violence (Section 3)

TypeIncludes
Physical AbuseHitting, slapping, burning, pushing, use of weapons, forced substance use
Sexual AbuseForced intercourse, forced pornography, humiliating sexual acts
Verbal/Emotional AbuseInsults, ridicule, name-calling, threats to harm/kill, isolation from family
Economic AbuseDenying access to finances, property, basic necessities; controlling money

Protections Available

  • Protection Order (S.18) – Restrain respondent from violence
  • Residence Order (S.19) – Right to remain in shared household
  • Monetary Relief (S.20) – Compensation for losses, expenses
  • Custody Order (S.21) – Temporary custody of children
⚠️ Therapist's Role: You are NOT a legal advisor but you MUST know your client's rights. If DV is disclosed: (1) Assess safety, (2) Provide information about services, (3) Document carefully, (4) Do NOT conduct conjoint therapy if active violence exists, (5) Refer to Protection Officer/NGO.

Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 – Key Provisions

Rights of Persons with Mental Illness

  • Right to Access Care (S.18) – Affordable, quality, near home
  • Right to Confidentiality (S.23) – Cannot disclose without consent
  • Informed Consent (S.22) – Must explain treatment, alternatives, risks
  • Advance Directive (S.5–12) – Patient can pre-decide treatment preferences
  • Right against cruel treatment (S.20) – No chaining, no forced isolation

Implications for Couple Therapists

  • RCI Registration: Must be registered with Rehabilitation Council of India to practice
  • Consent for each partner: Both must consent independently
  • Confidentiality limits: Cannot share one partner's individual disclosures without permission
  • Duty to report: Only if imminent risk of self-harm or harm to others
  • Record keeping: Maintain records for minimum 5 years
Key Point: The MHCA 2017 decriminalized suicide (S.115) – a client expressing suicidal ideation needs clinical support, not police involvement.

Section 498A IPC / BNS – Cruelty by Husband & Relatives

What the Law Says

  • S.498A IPC (now S.85/86 BNS): Cruelty by husband or his relatives
  • Cognizable, non-bailable, non-compoundable
  • Punishment: up to 3 years imprisonment + fine
  • Includes: dowry demands, mental cruelty, physical violence
  • Supreme Court guidelines (Rajesh Sharma, 2017): Family Welfare Committee referral before arrest

Implications for Therapists

  • Remain neutral – Do not take sides in legal disputes
  • Document carefully – Notes may be subpoenaed
  • Never provide legal advice – Refer to advocate
  • Understand context: Some clients are navigating 498A cases while in therapy
  • Both parties may be genuine: Avoid the "victim-perpetrator" trap
⚠️ Ethical Dilemma: If one partner files 498A, can therapy continue? Generally NO for conjoint work. Offer individual support. Resume only if legal counsel for both sides agrees and both partners consent.

Special Populations in Couple Therapy

10 Minutes

Premarital Counselling

Key Areas to Assess

Core Topics

  • Expectations – Roles, responsibilities, daily life vision
  • Living arrangements – Joint family vs. nuclear; city
  • Finances – Earning, saving, spending; joint vs. separate accounts
  • Family planning – When? How many? What if infertility?
  • In-law relationships – Boundaries, visits, decision-making
  • Conflict resolution style – How will disagreements be handled?

Evidence-Based Programs

  • PREPARE/ENRICH (Olson) – Most researched; 300+ items; identifies strength/growth areas
  • PREP (Prevention & Relationship Enhancement Program) – Communication & conflict skills
  • Indian adaptation needed: Include family system, cultural values, arranged marriage dynamics
💡 Tip: In arranged marriages, premarital counselling may be the ONLY space where partners can honestly discuss expectations before wedding.

Special Populations: Clinical Considerations

Universal Principle: Regardless of population, the core therapeutic stance remains: empathy, neutrality, cultural humility, and evidence-based practice.

Clinical Skills & Session Structure

10 Minutes

Standard 60-Minute Session Structure

PhaseTimeTasks
Opening0–10 minCheck-in with each partner; Review homework; Set session agenda collaboratively
Working Phase10–50 minInterventions (enactments, dialogue, restructuring); Process emotions; Practice new skills; Address cycle
Closing50–60 minSummarize key insights; Assign homework; Safety check; Schedule next session

Opening Phase Tips

  • Balance time equally between partners
  • Ask about homework FIRST (signals importance)
  • Note affect – who is distressed today?
  • Set agenda together (not just one partner's crisis)

Closing Phase Tips

  • Never open new emotional content after minute 50
  • End on a constructive note
  • Safety check: "Is everyone safe to go home together?"
  • Homework should be small, specific, achievable

Ethics in Couple Therapy

Confidentiality – The Secrets Dilemma

PolicyDescriptionPros/Cons
No-Secrets PolicyAnything told individually can be brought into conjoint session✅ Protects therapist; ❌ Partner may not disclose affairs
Full ConfidentialityIndividual disclosures kept completely private✅ Encourages honesty; ❌ Therapist holds secrets (collusion risk)
Limited ConfidentialityPrivate unless it directly harms therapy progress✅ Balanced; ❌ Therapist must make judgment calls

Other Ethical Considerations

  • Maintaining balance: Equal alliance with both partners (split alliance = therapy failure)
  • Dual relationships: Never treat a couple where you know one partner socially
  • When to recommend separation: Only when safety demands it (DV); otherwise, it's the couple's decision
  • Teletherapy ethics: Ensure both partners have private space; screen for DV; secure platforms only
  • Power imbalances: Gender, caste, economic – therapist must actively address
⚠️ Golden Rule: State your secrets policy CLEARLY in the first session and in the consent form. Changing policy mid-therapy = ethical violation.

Documentation in Couple Therapy

Consent Form Essentials

  • Nature of couple therapy explained
  • Confidentiality policy (including secrets policy)
  • Limits of confidentiality (harm, DV, court orders)
  • Session recording permission (if applicable)
  • Fees, cancellation policy, duration
  • Therapist's qualifications & RCI registration
  • Right to terminate at any time
  • Both partners must sign independently

SOAP Notes Format

  • S (Subjective): What each partner reported; presenting concerns this session
  • O (Objective): Therapist observations; affect; interaction patterns noted
  • A (Assessment): Progress toward goals; cycle activation; risk level
  • P (Plan): Next session focus; homework; referrals; safety plan updates

Record Keeping

  • Minimum 5 years retention (MHCA 2017)
  • Secure storage (physical lock / encrypted digital)
  • Risk assessment documented every session

Final Case: Create a Complete Treatment Plan

Case: Anita (34, teacher) and Vikram (38, IT professional), love marriage (inter-caste), 8 years, one child (5 years). Living nuclear but Vikram's parents in same city. Presenting complaint: Vikram discovered Anita's emotional affair (WhatsApp messages with college friend). Anita says: "He's been emotionally absent for 3 years. He works 14-hour days, comes home and watches phone. I felt invisible." Vikram says: "I trusted her completely. How can I ever trust again? My parents say I should divorce her." Vikram reports insomnia, weight loss, rage episodes. Anita reports guilt but also resentment. Both say "we want to try, for our child."

In your group (5 min), create:

  1. (a) Case formulation (attachment lens + systemic lens)
  2. (b) Treatment approach selection (which model? why?)
  3. (c) 3 measurable therapy goals
  4. (d) Session plan for first 4 sessions
Model Formulation:
Attachment: Vikram = anxious (betrayal injury activated hypervigilance); Anita = avoidant-turned-pursuer (sought connection externally when attachment needs unmet).
Cycle: Vikram withdraws into work → Anita feels abandoned → seeks connection elsewhere → Vikram discovers → rage/pursuit → Anita guilt/withdrawal.
Systemic: In-laws as triangulated voice pushing divorce. Child as stabilizing factor but also pressure.

Approach: EFT (attachment injury protocol) + Gottman Trust Revival Method
Goals: (1) Process attachment injury to restore safety, (2) Rebuild emotional accessibility & responsiveness, (3) Create new rituals of connection + work-life boundary
Sessions 1-4: S1: Individual intake (assess safety, Vikram's MH); S2: Individual (Anita - understand affair context); S3: Conjoint - present formulation, contract, secrets policy; S4: Map the cycle together, psychoeducation on attachment injury

The 10 Commandments of Couple Therapy

Key principles distilled from all 16 modules:

1. The relationship is your client – not either individual.
2. Assess for safety (DV, suicide) before EVERY intervention.
3. Understand the cycle – behaviour makes sense in context.
4. Access the emotion beneath the position – primary over secondary.
5. Cultural humility always – your textbook ≠ their lived reality.
6. Maintain balanced alliance – lose one partner, lose the therapy.
7. 69% of problems are perpetual – teach dialogue, not resolution.
8. Know the law, but never BE the lawyer.
9. Document everything – your notes may outlast the therapy.
10. Supervision is not optional – it's ethical practice.
Bonus: The 5:1 ratio applies to YOU too – ensure your therapeutic relationship has far more positive than negative moments for each partner.
