*Covers your request sections 7 (toolbox), 9 (scripts), 10 (Emotion Ladder).*

## 3.1 The Emotion Ladder (your request section 10)

A progression from basic awareness to adaptive coping. Use it as an **assessment and a map**: find the patient's current rung, teach the next one; don't skip rungs. Most alexithymic opioid-dependent patients enter around Level 1–2.

| Level | Skill | Alexithymia target | Session(s) | "You're on this rung when…" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Noticing bodily sensations | DIF (pre-req) | 2 | can report "chest tight," "restless" |
| 2 | Identifying basic emotions | DIF | 1–3 | can say "that's anger/sad" for a clear case |
| 3 | Labelling emotions (from a lexicon) | DDF | 3 | uses several distinct feeling words |
| 4 | Differentiating similar emotions | DIF/DDF | 3–4 | separates hurt from anger, worry from fear |
| 5 | Identifying intensity | DIF | 3–4 | rates 0–10; notices it changes |
| 6 | Identifying triggers | EOT/DIF | 4 | "X led to feeling Y" instead of "just happened" |
| 7 | Understanding emotional function | EOT | 4, 7 | "the fear was warning me / the anger about a boundary" |
| 8 | Identifying underlying (primary) emotions | DIF (deep) | 5, 7 | finds hurt/shame under anger/numbness |
| 9 | Identifying unmet needs | EOT | 5, 7 | "I was angry because I needed respect" |
| 10 | Expressing emotions adaptively | DDF | 5 | says "I feel… because… I need…" |
| 11 | Regulating emotions | regulation | 7–8 | self-soothes/urge-surfs instead of flooding |
| 12 | Adaptive coping instead of substances | outcome | 7–8 | uses a named response instead of using |

**How a patient progresses:** competence at a lower rung is the prerequisite for the next. You continuously *cycle back*: e.g., when at Level 8 (underlying emotion) you still use Level 1 (body) and Level 3 (labels). Movement is not strictly linear — expect a patient to be at Level 6 for anger but Level 2 for shame. Track per-emotion.

## 3.2 How to read each toolbox entry
Each technique gives: name · theoretical basis · purpose · emotional process targeted · alexithymia component · steps · therapist prompts · patient instructions · substance-use example · example worksheet (cross-ref Module 4) · common mistakes · when NOT to use · low-literacy modification · **evidence grade**.

I have only included techniques with a defensible theoretical or empirical basis, and I mark where a technique is **not distinctively EFT** (borrowed from DBT/RP/ACT/mindfulness) so you describe your intervention honestly.

---

### T1 — Emotion identification & labelling ("name it")
- **Basis:** EFT symbolising of bodily-felt experience; "affect labelling" research (putting feelings into words dampens amygdala reactivity). **Evidence: Moderate** (affect labelling has experimental support; mostly non-addiction samples).
- **Purpose / process:** convert felt arousal into a named emotion. **Targets: DIF.**
- **Steps:** (1) notice something is present; (2) locate in body; (3) offer 2–3 candidate words; (4) pick best fit; (5) check "does that fit?"
- **Therapist prompts:** "What's here right now?" · "If you had to guess — angry, sad, scared, or ashamed?" · "Which fits best?"
- **Patient instruction:** "When you notice something, try to put one word on it."
- **SUD example:** restlessness before a visit → "nervous/worried" about family reaction.
- **Worksheet:** W1, W6.
- **Common mistakes:** telling the patient what he feels; demanding precision too early; rushing.
- **Don't use when:** patient is flooded (regulate first).
- **Low-literacy:** face cards; point don't write.

### T2 — Emotion vocabulary building
- **Basis:** you can't label what you can't name; emotional granularity research (finer vocabulary → better regulation). **Evidence: Moderate** (granularity–regulation link), **Emerging** as an alexithymia intervention.
- **Purpose/process:** expand lexicon. **Targets: DDF** (then DIF).
- **Steps:** present families (mad/sad/scared/glad/shame) → patient selects usable words → build intensity ladders → attach to situations.
- **Prompts:** "Which words do you actually use? Which never?" · "A milder word than furious?"
- **SUD example:** replacing "stressed" with "overwhelmed / pressured / anxious / fed-up."
- **Worksheet:** W2, W3. **Mistakes:** overwhelming with long lists; academic words. **Don't use when:** N/A (foundational). **Low-literacy:** picture/face word bank, say aloud.

### T3 — Emotion differentiation
- **Basis:** granularity; EFT distinguishing emotions. **Evidence: Moderate/Emerging.**
- **Purpose/process:** split global "bad" into specific emotions. **Targets: DIF/DDF.**
- **Steps:** take a global word ("bad") → ask "which kind?" → contrast pairs (hurt vs angry; worried vs scared) → confirm by body cue + situation.
- **Prompts:** "Bad like heavy-sad, or bad like burning-angry, or bad like sick-scared?"
- **SUD example:** "I felt bad after the call" → hurt + rejected, not just "bad."
- **Worksheet:** W4. **Mistakes:** false precision; ignoring blends. **Low-literacy:** two face cards, "which is closer?"

### T4 — Focusing / bodily-felt sense (distinctively EFT)
- **Basis:** Gendlin's Focusing, central to EFT. **Evidence: Emerging** (some support in experiential therapy).
- **Purpose/process:** attend to the vague bodily "felt sense," let a word/image emerge. **Targets: DIF, EOT.**
- **Steps:** settle → notice the body → find the unclear sense → ask "what's this about?" → let a word/image come → check it against the body.
- **Prompts:** "Put your attention in the middle of your body… what's the feel of this whole thing?" · "Does the word 'trapped' fit? Check with your body."
- **SUD example:** a heavy, stuck sense resolving into "trapped / hopeless."
- **Worksheet:** W5-based. **Mistakes:** rushing; over-talking; using with dissociation-prone/trauma-activated patients without stabilisation. **Don't use when:** active dissociation, high trauma load, flooding — ground instead. **Low-literacy:** fine (non-verbal), guide gently.

### T5 — Body-sensation mapping / interoceptive awareness
- **Basis:** interoception underlies emotion; alexithymia linked to interoceptive deficits. **Evidence: Emerging.**
- **Purpose/process:** map where emotions register bodily. **Targets: DIF.** **Steps/prompts/example:** see Session 2. **Worksheet:** W5. **Don't use when:** trauma-activating; keep eyes open, brief. **Low-literacy:** colour a body outline.

### T6 — Emotion tracking / diary
- **Basis:** self-monitoring (transtheoretical; also CBT — **not uniquely EFT**). **Evidence: Moderate** (monitoring improves awareness).
- **Purpose/process:** capture feelings + triggers over time. **Targets: DIF, EOT.** **Worksheet:** W8, W20. **Mistakes:** too many columns; abstract. **Low-literacy:** faces + tally.

### T7 — Trigger identification (event→emotion link)
- **Basis:** functional analysis (RP/CBT — **borrowed, not uniquely EFT**), plus EFT situational appraisal. **Evidence: Moderate** (core to relapse prevention).
- **Purpose/process:** link external event to internal state. **Targets: EOT, DIF.** **Worksheet:** W7, W8, W17. **Example:** payday text → shame → craving.

### T8 — Emotion-function work ("what is this feeling for?")
- **Basis:** EFT (emotions as adaptive information). **Evidence: Theoretical.**
- **Purpose/process:** build psychological mindedness. **Targets: EOT.**
- **Prompts:** "If this fear were trying to protect you, from what?" · "What is the anger telling you matters?"
- **SUD example:** loneliness signalling an unmet need for connection. **Mistakes:** intellectualising it (keep it experiential).

### T9 — Underlying / primary-emotion work (distinctively EFT)
- **Basis:** EFT primary vs secondary emotion. **Evidence: Emerging/Theoretical.**
- **Purpose/process:** reach the primary emotion beneath a secondary one. **Targets: DIF (deep).**
- **Prompts:** "Anger makes sense — often there's something softer underneath. Any hurt or fear there too?"
- **SUD example:** rage at partner → underneath, fear of abandonment + shame. **Don't use when:** not stabilised; too early in the 8 sessions; flooding risk. **Safety:** titrate; this is the deepest rung you'll touch in a brief skills course.

### T10 — Unmet-need identification (distinctively EFT)
- **Basis:** EFT emotion→need link. **Evidence: Theoretical.**
- **Purpose/process:** connect emotion to the need it signals. **Targets: EOT.**
- **Prompts:** "If that hurt could ask for something, what would it need?" **Worksheet:** W12. **SUD example:** "I needed to feel respected / safe / wanted."

### T11 — Emotional expression skills ("I feel… because… I need…")
- **Basis:** EFT expression + assertive-communication (partly borrowed). **Evidence: Moderate** (assertion/communication training).
- **Purpose/process:** communicate a feeling. **Targets: DDF.** **Worksheet:** W14, W18. **See Session 5.**

### T12 — Emotional acceptance / reducing experiential avoidance
- **Basis:** EFT allowing/tolerating emotion; overlaps ACC/ACT (**partly borrowed**). **Evidence: Moderate** (acceptance-based approaches).
- **Purpose/process:** approach rather than avoid feelings. **Targets: EOT.**
- **Prompts:** "Can we let it be here for a moment, without pushing it away or fixing it?" **Worksheet:** W13. **Don't use when:** overwhelming affect (pair with grounding).

### T13 — Self-soothing / grounding (borrowed: DBT/mindfulness)
- **Basis:** DBT distress tolerance / grounding — **NOT EFT; label it as a stabilisation add-on.** **Evidence: Moderate** (DBT skills).
- **Purpose/process:** down-regulate arousal so emotion is tolerable and processing is possible. **Targets: regulation (enables DIF).**
- **Steps:** feet on floor, slow exhale-longer-than-inhale, name 5 things seen, cool water; self-kind phrase. **Worksheet:** W15, W19. **Use:** any time arousal/craving climbs.

### T14 — Distress tolerance / urge-surfing (borrowed: DBT/RP/mindfulness)
- **Basis:** RP/mindfulness urge-surfing; distress tolerance predicts abstinence (Berking 2011). **Evidence: Moderate.**
- **Purpose/process:** ride an emotion/craving wave without acting. **Targets: regulation, coping.** **Worksheet:** W9, W16. **See Session 7.** **NOT EFT** — say so.

### T15 — Chair work (empty-chair / two-chair) — distinctively EFT, USE WITH CAUTION
- **Basis:** classic EFT experiential technique. **Evidence: Moderate** for EFT generally; **not evaluated in brief addiction skills training.**
- **Purpose/process:** two-chair for self-criticism/conflict; empty-chair for unresolved feelings toward another. **Targets: deep DIF, primary emotion, unmet need.**
- **When NOT to use (important):** In your 8-session brief protocol with post-detox, shame-prone, possibly trauma-loaded, possibly HIV-affected inpatients, **full chair work is generally contraindicated as a routine component** — it can rapidly access intense affect you don't have sessions to process safely, and risks destabilisation. **Recommendation:** do NOT build chair work into the core protocol. If a highly stable patient spontaneously reaches a marker and your supervisor agrees, a *brief, well-grounded* empty-chair expression may be used — but it should not be standard, and any use must be logged and supervised. Prioritise stabilisation and skills.
- **Low-literacy:** verbal, no reading needed — literacy is not the barrier; affect tolerance is.

### T16 — Alternative-response building / substitution
- **Basis:** RP coping planning (**borrowed**) + EFT (new adaptive response). **Evidence: Moderate.**
- **Purpose/process:** replace drug-as-regulator with a named response. **Targets: coping (Level 12).** **Worksheet:** W16, W17. **See Session 7.**

## 3.3 Therapist scripts (your request section 9)

These model *slowing down, scaffolding, going under the secondary emotion, and handling resistance*. Adapt to the patient's language; deliver in his preferred language (Hindi/local) in practice.

### Script A — The core "unpacking 'bad'" sequence
> **T:** "When you say you felt bad, can we slow down and see what 'bad' means?"
> **P:** "Just bad. I don't know."
> **T:** "That's okay — most of us never learned the words. Let's start with the body. When you felt bad, where did you notice it — chest, stomach, head, shoulders?"
> **P:** "Chest. Kind of tight."
> **T:** "Tight chest. Good noticing. Tight chest often comes with worry, or anger, or feeling hurt. Which of those feels closest?"
> **P:** "Maybe… angry."
> **T:** "Angry. And sometimes under anger there's something softer — feeling let down, or disrespected. Was any of that there?"
> **P:** "Yeah… he disrespected me in front of everyone."
> **T:** "So the 'bad' was anger, and underneath, feeling disrespected and hurt. That's a lot clearer than 'bad,' isn't it? What did the hurt make you want?"
> **P:** "For him to respect me."
> **T:** "Right — you needed respect. See how we went from 'bad' to a real feeling and a real need? That's the skill."

### Script B — Patient says "I don't know."
> **T:** "How did you feel when she said she's leaving?"
> **P:** "I don't know."
> **T:** "'I don't know' is a fine honest answer — feelings are hard to catch. Let's not go for the word yet. What did your body do in that moment?"
> **P:** "Went cold. Empty."
> **T:** "Cold and empty. If I offered three words — scared, sad, numb — does any land?"
> **P:** "Numb. Then later, sad."
> **T:** "So first numb, then sad — that's two feelings, one after another. You just did it."

### Script C — "Nothing." / "I'm fine."
> **P:** "I feel nothing. I'm fine."
> **T:** "That's allowed — sometimes 'nothing' is what's there, and sometimes 'nothing' is the body's way of switching off something too big. No pressure either way. If the 'nothing' had a texture — flat, empty, heavy, calm — which is it?"
> **P:** "Empty. Flat."
> **T:** "Empty and flat. That's actually a feeling-state, not nothing. Shall we just notice it together for a moment?"

### Script D — "I was just angry." (single undifferentiated label)
> **T:** "Anger's clear and real. Anger's often like the tip — there's usually something under it. Just before the anger, was there a flash of hurt, or fear, or shame?"
> **P:** "I don't do shame."
> **T:** "Fair enough. What about — did it feel like someone crossed a line, or like something was taken from you, or like you were pushed aside?"
> **P:** "Pushed aside. Like I don't matter."
> **T:** "So under the anger — 'I don't matter.' That's important. That's the feeling we can actually work with."

### Script E — "My body was restless." (somatic only)
> **T:** "Restless body — good, you noticed it. Let's use it. Restless often goes with anxious, or with craving, or with anger with nowhere to go. When it came, what had just happened?"
> **P:** "Nothing. Just sitting."
> **T:** "Sitting still, and restless rises. Sometimes when we stop moving, feelings we've outrun catch up. If the restlessness could talk, is it more 'I'm on edge/worried' or more 'I want to escape something'?"
> **P:** "Escape. I wanted to use."
> **T:** "So restless → wanting to escape → craving. What might you have been trying to escape *from* — boredom, loneliness, a thought?"

### Script F — "I wanted to use." (craving substituted for emotion)
> **T:** "Okay — the urge to use was strong. Let's treat the urge like a smoke alarm: it's telling us something's burning. Right *before* the urge, what feeling was there?"
> **P:** "Just the urge."
> **T:** "Let's rewind ten minutes. Where were you, what happened, and what was your body doing?"
> **P:** "After my mother called. Crying on the phone. My chest got heavy."
> **T:** "Heavy chest after your mother cried. That heavy is often guilt or sadness. So: mother cries → guilt/sadness → heavy chest → urge to switch it off with using. The feeling came first. That's the one we learn to handle."

### Script G — "I don't think about feelings." (EOT)
> **T:** "That's honest, and pretty common — a lot of people run on doing, not feeling. I'm not asking you to become someone who talks about feelings all day. Think of it like a mechanic learning one more gauge on the dashboard. If you could read this one gauge, you'd get earlier warning before things blow up — including before you use. Willing to try just the gauge?"

### Script H — "Talking about emotions won't help me."
> **T:** "You might be right that talking alone doesn't fix things — I agree, actually. This isn't just talking; it's a practical skill, like learning to spot a red light before the crash. The test is simple: try it for a couple of sessions and see if it helps you catch cravings earlier. If it's useless, you tell me and we change course. Deal?"

### Script I — "I can't explain it."
> **T:** "You don't have to explain it well — broken words are fine. Give me the messy version, or even a picture. 'It's like…' — finish that any way you want."
> **P:** "It's like… a stone in my chest."
> **T:** "A stone in the chest. That's a great description. Heavy, hard, pressing down. That sounds like grief or guilt to me — does either fit the stone?"

### Script J — "There's nothing to talk about."
> **T:** "That's okay — we don't need a big story. Let's just do a 60-second check: how's your body right now, and pick one word off this card. That's all. Small is fine here."

### Handling resistance — general pattern
1. **Validate the resistance** ("makes sense," "you're not wrong").
2. **Reduce the demand** (smaller step, body not words, a choice of 3).
3. **Reconnect to his goal** (less conflict, staying out, sleeping, family).
4. **Offer a test, not a belief** ("try it twice, judge for yourself").
5. **Never argue or shame.** Curiosity beats confrontation with this population.

### Moving deeper (the general depth move)
Body cue → tentative label → check fit → differentiate → look just under a secondary emotion → find the need → (regulate) → optionally express. Only go as deep as the patient stays regulated. **Depth is not the goal in a brief skills course — reliable naming and regulation are.**
