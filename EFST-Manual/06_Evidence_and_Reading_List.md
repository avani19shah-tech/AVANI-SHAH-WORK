## 6.1 THE INTERVENTION MANUAL (one-page synthesis) — your request section 14

**Title:** EFT-informed Emotion-Focused Skills Training (EFST) to reduce alexithymia in opioid-dependent inpatients.

- **Rationale (short):** Alexithymia is common in opioid dependence and is theorised to maintain use by leaving negative affect unnamed and unregulated (self-medication). A structured curriculum that teaches noticing → naming → differentiating → understanding → expressing → regulating emotion should raise emotional awareness and thereby lower alexithymia (primarily DIF/DDF). Directly testing this is your contribution; the evidence for the packaged claim is **Emerging**.
- **Theoretical model:** EFT emotion theory (Greenberg) + Lane & Schwartz levels of emotional awareness + Khantzian self-medication + interoception/affect-labelling research.
- **Target population:** male opioid-dependent inpatients, 18–50, post-detox (≥7 days), basic literacy, consenting; HIV+ and HIV− (your two groups).
- **Inclusion considerations:** medically stable, past acute withdrawal, able to attend 8 sessions.
- **Exclusion / safety:** active psychosis, IQ<70, active neurological disorder / HIV-associated dementia (3–4), acute medical instability, acute high suicidality (stabilise/refer first), primary non-opioid dependence, recent (≤6 mo) emotion-regulation training.
- **Therapist competencies:** trained clinical psychology (MPhil trainee under qualified supervision), basic EFT/emotion-skills familiarity, risk assessment, grounding/stabilisation skills, cultural and language competence (Hindi/local), and ongoing supervision.
- **Dose/structure:** 8 individual sessions, 60 min, 2×/week × 4 weeks; same core content/dose for both groups.
- **Sequence:** S1 psychoeducation → S2 body–emotion → S3 vocabulary → S4 identify-in-daily-life/triggers → S5 express → S6 read others → S7 emotions & addiction cycle → S8 integration/maintenance. (Details: Module 2.)
- **Therapist instructions / patient activities / worksheets / homework:** Modules 2–4.
- **Progress monitoring:** session behavioural anchors + patient progress checklist + TAS-20/DERS pre-post + RCI (Module 5).
- **Treatment fidelity:** per-session fidelity checklist; log all adaptations; keep content/dose identical across groups (Module 5.5).
- **Adaptations — low literacy:** faces/body-outline/verbal/scribed worksheets (Module 4 low-literacy track).
- **Adaptations — HIV:** identical core; adapt *pace/support* not *content/dose*; process HIV-related emotions with the standard skills when the patient raises them; lower referral threshold; absolute confidentiality (Module 2.4).
- **Adaptations — alexithymia severity:** severe → spend longer on Ladder Levels 1–3 (body/naming/vocabulary), less on depth; milder → progress faster to expression/needs.
- **Distress/craving management:** grounding taught S2; craving protocol + overwhelming-affect protocol (Module 5.9).

## 6.2A Theoretical rationale (concise) — your request 16A

Opioid-dependent individuals show high rates of alexithymia, and difficulty **identifying feelings (DIF)** in particular has the strongest association with substance use in meta-analysis (Westwood et al., 2023). If, as the self-medication hypothesis proposes, opioids are recruited to manage undifferentiated, unbearable affect, then the *inability to symbolise emotion* is not incidental but functional to the addiction. EFT holds that emotions become workable only once they are reached and put into words ("arrive before you leave"), and that emotional competence is trainable. An EFT-informed skills curriculum that systematically rebuilds the sensation→label→meaning→expression→regulation chain should therefore raise emotional awareness and reduce alexithymia — most plausibly its DIF and DDF components — and, by giving the person a way to name and regulate affect other than the drug, weaken the functional pull toward use. Because no study has yet tested this package in opioid-dependent adults (your gap), the rationale is **theoretically strong but empirically Emerging**, which is exactly why the study is worth doing.

## 6.2B Conceptual model — with critique and an improved version — your request 16B

**Your proposed linear model:**
EFST → improved emotional awareness → improved identification/differentiation → improved expression/understanding → reduced emotional avoidance/maladaptive coping → improved emotional regulation → reduced alexithymia.

**Critique (what an examiner will probe):**
1. **Circularity risk.** "Reduced alexithymia" and "improved identification/differentiation/expression" are *largely the same construct* (DIF/DDF/EOT). The model risks defining the outcome as the mechanism. Fix: treat identification/differentiation/expression as the **operational content of alexithymia reduction** (the DV), and place *distinct* mechanisms (interoceptive re-training, affect labelling, vocabulary acquisition, reduced experiential avoidance, corrective relational experience) as the **mediators**.
2. **Linearity is too clean.** The real process is **iterative and bidirectional**: regulation enables awareness (you can only attend to a feeling you can tolerate), and awareness enables regulation. Arrows should loop.
3. **Missing moderators.** HIV status (your whole question), baseline alexithymia severity, depression (BDI-II), dependence severity (SDS), literacy/verbal ability, and concurrent standard care all moderate or confound the path. They belong in the model.
4. **EOT is mis-placed.** The model implies EOT moves with the rest; evidence says EOT is the most stable and may **not** move in 8 sessions. Show it as a slower/dashed path.
5. **Outcome overreach.** Don't let the model imply "→ reduced relapse"; you don't measure it and the evidence doesn't support asserting it.

**Improved model (describe it this way in your thesis):**

```
                 EFST curriculum (8 sessions)
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
        ▼                 ▼                             ▼
 Interoceptive      Affect-labelling +          Reduced experiential
 re-training        vocabulary acquisition       avoidance + corrective
 (mediators)                                      relational experience
        └───────┬─────────┴───────────────┬─────────────┘
                ▼                          ▼
        ↓ DIF, ↓ DDF   ⇄  ↑ emotion regulation (DERS↓)   [bidirectional loop]
                │                          
                ▼                          (EOT: slower / may not change — dashed)
        ↓ TAS-20 total  ────────────────────────────────►  (distal, NOT tested here:
                ▲                                            ↓ craving/relapse — hypothesised only)
   Moderators/confounds: HIV status • baseline severity • BDI-II • SDS •
   literacy/verbal ability • concurrent standard care
```

Key improvements: mechanisms are **distinct from** the outcome; the awareness⇄regulation link is a **loop**; **EOT** is shown as slower/uncertain; **moderators/confounds** (esp. HIV) are explicit; the **relapse** arrow is marked hypothesised and untested.
