# Module 2 — The 8-Session EFST Protocol

*Covers your request sections 4 (session-by-session), 5 (opioid adaptations), 6 (HIV considerations).*

This operationalises the exact 8 session titles already in your synopsis (slide 13), so your manual and your proposal stay consistent. Format is fixed to your design: **individual, 60 minutes, 2×/week over 4 weeks, delivered to both HIV+ and HIV− groups with the same core protocol.**

---

## 2.0 Design logic — why this sequence (progressive, not random)

The sequence follows the **Emotion Ladder** (Module 3) and the Lane & Schwartz levels of emotional awareness (body → action → single feeling → differentiated feelings → blends). It moves from **safest and most concrete** to **more emotionally demanding**, so that stabilisation and skill precede any deeper processing:

1–2: **Foundation & interoception** (safe, psychoeducational, body-based) → mostly DIF groundwork.
3–4: **Language & self-identification** (vocabulary, then apply to own daily life) → DDF then DIF.
5–6: **Communication & other-awareness** (express to others, read others) → DDF and social-emotional, chips at EOT.
7: **Integration into the addiction cycle** (the "so what for my using" session) → EOT + regulation + relapse-linked.
8: **Consolidation & maintenance** (portable plan for after discharge) → all three, transfer.

**Progression rule:** each session opens by using the previous session's homework as raw material. You never teach a skill in isolation; Session N always *applies* skills from Sessions 1…N−1.

A visual map (Session → Ladder level → primary alexithymia target) is in `06_Evidence_and_Reading_List.md` (Table C).

## 2.1 Global therapist stance (applies to every session)

- **Relationship first.** Warm, unhurried, non-judgemental, collaborative. With this population, shame and stigma are high; a single moralising comment can end engagement.
- **Scaffold, don't lead.** Offer *choices* of feeling words ("more angry, or more hurt, or more scared?") rather than telling the patient what he feels. Leading contaminates your own outcome measure.
- **Validate before exploring.** "That makes sense" before "let's look closer."
- **Titrate affect.** Your job in an 8-session skills course is *building capacity*, not opening deep trauma. Keep arousal in the tolerable "window." Stabilise > excavate.
- **Concrete and short.** Low concentration and literacy are common post-detox. Short sentences, one idea at a time, lots of repetition, visual aids.
- **End regulated.** Every session ends calmer than its middle (grounding + a small win + clear homework).

## 2.2 Materials used throughout
Feelings-word list + **emotion face cards** (for low literacy), body outline sheet, 0–10 intensity ruler (visual), the session's worksheet (Module 4), a folder for the patient to keep sheets, and your **fidelity checklist + session monitoring sheet** (Module 5). Have water and a grounding script to hand for distress/craving.

# SESSION 1 — Psychoeducation: What Are Emotions? Why They Matter

1. **Session number:** 1
2. **Title:** What are emotions, and why do they matter? (Orientation + rationale + safety)
3. **Main objective:** Build alliance, give a simple shared model of emotions, and create motivation to attend to inner experience.
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) explain purpose of EFST in the patient's terms; (b) normalise that emotions are useful signals; (c) introduce the idea that feelings show up in the *body*; (d) get a first, low-threat emotion self-report; (e) set collaborative expectations and confidentiality.
5. **Skills developed:** basic emotional literacy; approach (vs avoidance) attitude to feelings; orientation to the body–feeling idea. (Emotion Ladder Level 1–2.)
6. **Duration:** 60 min.
7. **Therapist preparation:** Review chart (drug history, detox day, HIV status *privately*, literacy, current mood/withdrawal, risk). Prepare face cards and the "Emotions are signals" handout. Confirm the patient is medically stable and past acute withdrawal.
8. **Opening / rapport check-in (5–8 min):** Introduce yourself and role. "This is not about your drug use as such — it's about feelings, and how noticing them can make life and staying well easier. There are no right answers and no marks." Ask how the day/night has been (sleep, restlessness) — this doubles as a state check.
9. **Psychoeducation (12–15 min):** Emotions = fast body-signals that tell us something matters and prepare us to act (fear→protect, anger→a line was crossed, sadness→a loss, happiness→something good). They are *information*, not weakness. Many people who use substances learned to switch feelings off — useful once, costly now. Skills can be re-learned. Use one everyday, non-drug example (e.g., anger when someone jumps a queue).
10. **Exact activities:** (a) "Signals" mini-teach with face cards; (b) "Where do *you* feel things?" — quick pointing to the body; (c) first use of the 0–10 "how strong" ruler on a neutral recent feeling.
11. **Step-by-step procedure:** (1) rationale + confidentiality; (2) teach the signals idea with cards; (3) ask for one recent ordinary feeling (not drug-related): name it together; (4) locate it in the body; (5) rate 0–10; (6) praise any attempt; (7) set homework; (8) grounding + close.
12. **Therapist language/questions:** "What made today a bit better or worse?" · "If a feeling were a signal, like a horn or a light, what might this one be telling you?" · "Where in your body did you notice that?" · "No wrong answers here."
13. **Patient practice:** name one non-drug feeling, locate it, rate it, in session, with help.
14. **Worksheet:** *W1 Emotion identification* + *W6 "What am I feeling?"* (Module 4) introduced.
15. **Homework:** once a day, notice **one** feeling, and just mark a face on the card sheet + where in the body. (Keep it tiny.)
16. **Expected difficulties:** "I don't have feelings / what's the point"; only reports craving or "restless"; suspicious of the exercise; poor concentration.
17. **Responding to difficulties:** normalise ("many people here say that at first"); accept "restless" as a valid *body* signal and build from it; reduce demand ("just one feeling, one time a day"); keep it concrete; never argue.
18. **How it targets alexithymia:** plants the DIF/EOT seeds — that inner states exist, are namable, and are worth attending to; begins reversing externally-oriented avoidance by making a *tiny* inward turn feel safe.
19. **Expected short-term outcome:** patient can, with help, name and locate one non-drug feeling and agrees to a small daily notice-task.
20. **Progression criteria:** attends, tolerates the topic, completes (even partially) one self-observation → proceed to S2. If flooded or highly suspicious, spend more of S2 on rapport/stabilisation before body work.

# SESSION 2 — The Body–Emotion Connection (Interoceptive Awareness)

1. **Session number:** 2
2. **Title:** Feelings live in the body — noticing bodily signals.
3. **Main objective:** Strengthen interoceptive awareness and start mapping bodily sensations to emotions (core DIF work).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) review homework; (b) teach a short, safe body-scan; (c) build a personal "body map" of sensations; (d) begin pairing sensations with tentative emotion words.
5. **Skills developed:** interoception; sensation→emotion linking; basic self-soothing/grounding (taught as a safety tool). (Ladder Level 1→2.)
6. **Duration:** 60 min.
7. **Preparation:** body-outline sheets, coloured pens; a 3–4 min grounding/breathing script; note any trauma history (keep body work light if trauma-loaded — see Module 5 safety).
8. **Check-in (5–8 min):** review the daily notice-homework warmly; pick ONE example to work with.
9. **Psychoeducation (8–10 min):** "Before we have a *word* for a feeling, the body already reacts — heart speeds up, chest tightens, stomach knots, shoulders lift. Learning to read the body is step one to knowing what you feel." Explicitly connect to withdrawal/craving: "restlessness and that 'crawling' feeling are also body-signals — we can learn to read those too."
10. **Exact activities:** (a) brief guided body-scan (eyes open if eyes-closed is unsafe/uncomfortable); (b) *Body map* (W5): colour where sensations show up; (c) sensation→emotion pairing using the word list/cards.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) teach + do a short body-scan; (3) patient marks current sensations on the body outline; (4) for one sensation, offer 2–3 candidate emotions; (5) rate intensity 0–10; (6) teach one grounding skill (feet on floor, slow breath) as a "volume knob"; (7) HW; (8) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "As you sit here, what does your body notice — anywhere tight, heavy, hot, restless, empty?" · "If that tight chest could speak, is it more worried, more angry, or more sad?" · "Let's turn the volume down together before we finish."
13. **Patient practice:** complete a body map; pair at least one sensation with a candidate emotion; practise grounding once in session.
14. **Worksheet:** *W5 Body sensations and emotions*.
15. **Homework:** once daily, a 1-minute body check → mark one sensation + a guess at the feeling (W5 mini-version).
16. **Difficulties:** "I feel nothing in my body"; only somatic ("just restless/gastric"); body-scan triggers anxiety or trauma flashes; dissociation.
17. **Responding:** start with the strongest/most obvious sensation, even discomfort; accept somatic reports as the *raw material*, don't push for a word yet; keep eyes open, shorten or stop the scan and ground if arousal climbs; **if trauma activation appears, stop the interoceptive exercise, ground, and note for supervision** (Module 5).
18. **Targets alexithymia:** directly trains the sensation→label pathway that is broken in DIF; begins converting pure somatic experience (the classic alexithymic/somatising presentation) into emotional meaning.
19. **Short-term outcome:** patient identifies ≥1 bodily sensation and offers a tentative feeling word for it, and can use one grounding skill.
20. **Progression criteria:** can locate sensations and tolerate the exercise without significant destabilisation → S3.

# SESSION 3 — Building an Emotion Vocabulary

1. **Number:** 3
2. **Title:** Words for feelings — building your emotion vocabulary.
3. **Main objective:** Expand the patient's usable feeling-word repertoire (core DDF work).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) move beyond "good/bad/tension/fine"; (b) group words into families (mad/sad/scared/glad/shame); (c) attach words to remembered situations and body cues.
5. **Skills developed:** emotion labelling, vocabulary, first differentiation. (Ladder Level 3.)
6. **Duration:** 60 min.
7. **Preparation:** feelings-word list organised by family and by intensity; face cards; the patient's body maps from S2.
8. **Check-in:** review body-check HW; carry one example forward.
9. **Psychoeducation (8 min):** "We can only name a feeling if we have the word. Most of us grew up with two or three feeling words. Today we grow your collection — like adding tools." Introduce **families** (angry, sad, scared, happy, ashamed) and that each has *mild→strong* versions (annoyed→furious).
10. **Activities:** (a) sort face/word cards into families; (b) intensity ladder within one family (irritated→annoyed→angry→furious); (c) attach one word to a real recent situation + its body cue.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) present families with cards; (3) patient picks words that "sound like him"; (4) build one intensity ladder; (5) link a word to a situation + body sensation from his own week; (6) HW; (7) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "Which of these words do you actually use? Which do you never use?" · "Between 'annoyed' and 'furious', where was you last night?" · "Pick two new words to try this week."
13. **Patient practice:** produce/recognise ≥5 feeling words across ≥3 families; place one word on an intensity ladder.
14. **Worksheet:** *W2 Emotion vocabulary* + *W3 Emotion intensity rating*.
15. **Homework:** use a *feelings word of the day* (pick from the list) and note when it fit.
16. **Difficulties:** limited literacy; "these are women's words / not for me"; picks only "angry" for everything; embarrassment.
17. **Responding:** use faces/pictures and say words aloud for low literacy; de-gender ("strong men feel all of these — naming them is a skill, like driving"); if everything is "angry," gently probe whether some was hurt/worried/ashamed; keep it light and even playful.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** directly builds the lexicon DDF requires and seeds differentiation for DIF; a bigger vocabulary is a precondition for every later session.
19. **Short-term outcome:** measurable growth in feeling-word range used in session vs S1.
20. **Progression criteria:** can name several distinct feelings and grade intensity → S4.

# SESSION 4 — Identifying Feelings in Daily Life (Self-Identification + Triggers)

1. **Number:** 4
2. **Title:** Catching feelings as they happen — triggers and self-identification.
3. **Main objective:** Apply naming/differentiation to the patient's *own* real daily situations, and link feelings to triggers (DIF + starts EOT).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) use the trigger→body→feeling→urge chain; (b) differentiate similar feelings in real examples; (c) notice the link between events and inner states.
5. **Skills:** emotion tracking, differentiation, trigger identification, event→emotion linking. (Ladder Level 4–6.)
6. **Duration:** 60 min.
7. **Preparation:** the *Emotion–thought–behaviour* and *trigger diary* worksheets; patient's prior sheets.
8. **Check-in:** review word-of-the-day HW; select a real incident from the ward/week.
9. **Psychoeducation (7 min):** introduce the simple chain: **Something happens → body reacts → there's a feeling → there's an urge to do something.** "If we can catch the feeling, we get a choice about the urge."
10. **Activities:** (a) walk one real incident through the chain together; (b) differentiate ("was that anger, or hurt under the anger?"); (c) identify the trigger explicitly.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) pick one concrete recent event; (3) fill the chain (trigger→body→feeling→urge) collaboratively; (4) test 2–3 candidate feelings, pick best fit; (5) name the trigger; (6) HW: keep a trigger diary; (7) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "Let's rewind to just before you felt that — what happened first?" · "Your fists tightened — anger fits; was there also something softer underneath, like feeling disrespected or let down?" · "What did the feeling make you want to do?"
13. **Patient practice:** complete ≥1 full chain in session; commit to logging triggers.
14. **Worksheet:** *W7 Emotion–thought–behaviour connection* + *W8 Emotional trigger diary*.
15. **Homework:** log 2–3 trigger→feeling→urge episodes over the week.
16. **Difficulties:** "it just happened, no reason"; jumps to the urge/craving and skips the feeling; overwhelmed recalling a charged event.
17. **Responding:** treat "no reason" as "reason not yet found" and slow the replay down; when he jumps to "I wanted to use," ask "and just *before* the wanting — what was the feeling?"; keep examples low-intensity early; ground if needed.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** builds DIF in vivo (real self-referential identification, not abstract) and directly attacks EOT by forcing an inner-cause link where the habit is external-only narration.
19. **Short-term outcome:** patient completes a trigger→feeling→urge chain with a differentiated feeling.
20. **Progression criteria:** can self-identify a feeling tied to a trigger, with reduced "I don't know" → S5.

# SESSION 5 — Describing Feelings to Others (Expression)

1. **Number:** 5
2. **Title:** Putting feelings into words to another person.
3. **Main objective:** Practise *expressing* an identified feeling to someone (core DDF, now interpersonal).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) turn an inner state into a spoken sentence; (b) learn a simple "I feel… because… I need…" frame; (c) reduce fear/shame about expressing.
5. **Skills:** emotional expression, interpersonal emotional communication, unmet-need identification (intro). (Ladder Level 9–10.)
6. **Duration:** 60 min.
7. **Preparation:** the *healthy expression* and *interpersonal communication* worksheets; a role-play plan using a real relationship (family, partner, ward staff).
8. **Check-in:** review trigger diary; pick one episode involving another person.
9. **Psychoeducation (7 min):** "Feelings kept fully inside often leak out as anger, silence, or using. Saying a feeling in words — even simply — lowers the pressure and helps people understand you." Teach the frame: **"I feel ___ because ___; what I need is ___."**
10. **Activities:** (a) build one such sentence from his real episode; (b) role-play saying it (you play the other person); (c) introduce the buried need under the feeling.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) pick a real interpersonal episode; (3) identify feeling + need; (4) craft the sentence; (5) rehearse aloud (role-play/rehearsal); (6) debrief how it felt to say it; (7) HW; (8) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "If you could say the feeling instead of showing it, what would the sentence be?" · "Underneath the anger at your wife, what did you need from her?" · "Let's practise — I'll be her."
13. **Patient practice:** speak ≥1 "I feel… because… I need…" sentence aloud in session.
14. **Worksheet:** *W14 Healthy emotional expression* + *W18 Interpersonal emotional communication* + *W12 Unmet needs* (intro).
15. **Homework:** notice one situation where he *could* express a feeling in words; optionally try one low-risk expression.
16. **Difficulties:** "men don't talk like that"; fears expression = weakness or conflict; can identify but freezes when speaking; anger flares in role-play.
17. **Responding:** reframe expression as strength/control, not weakness; start with the *safest* relationship and lowest-stakes feeling; if anger flares, pause, ground, name the softer feeling under it; keep sentences short.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** the definitional DDF task — converting inner state to communicable words to another person; also chips EOT by requiring reflection on need.
19. **Short-term outcome:** patient produces and voices a feeling-statement.
20. **Progression criteria:** can put a feeling into a sentence aloud → S6.

# SESSION 6 — Understanding Others' Emotions (Empathy / Social Emotional Awareness)

1. **Number:** 6
2. **Title:** Reading feelings in others.
3. **Main objective:** Extend emotional awareness outward — recognise and interpret others' emotions (social-emotional skill; indirectly strengthens self-labelling and reduces EOT).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) read emotion cues in faces/tone/situation; (b) infer likely feelings and needs of others; (c) connect this to fewer interpersonal misunderstandings (a relapse driver).
5. **Skills:** other-emotion recognition, perspective-taking, interpersonal understanding. (Supports Ladder Levels 3–4 and 9.)
6. **Preparation:** face-emotion cards/photos; short neutral vignettes (ward/family scenarios), avoiding graphic trauma content.
7. **Duration:** 60 min.
8. **Check-in:** review expression HW.
9. **Psychoeducation (7 min):** "Feelings are also signals *between* people. Missing them causes fights and misunderstanding; reading them helps relationships — and stress from relationships is a big trigger for using."
10. **Activities:** (a) label emotions in face cards/photos; (b) a vignette: "what might he be feeling, and what might he need?"; (c) link a real past conflict to a probable misread emotion.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) face-reading warm-up; (3) vignette inference (feeling + need + cue); (4) apply to one real relationship conflict; (5) HW; (6) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "What do you see in this face — the eyes, the mouth?" · "If your father went quiet like that, what might he have been feeling underneath?" · "How might reading that earlier have changed things?"
13. **Patient practice:** correctly label several facial/contextual emotions and infer one person's feeling+need in a vignette.
14. **Worksheet:** *W18 Interpersonal emotional communication* (other-focused section).
15. **Homework:** observe one person daily and guess their feeling + one cue that told you.
16. **Difficulties:** poor facial-emotion recognition (documented in heroin users); over-attribution of hostility; "I can't know what others feel."
17. **Responding:** teach specific cues (brows, mouth, posture, tone); test hostile attributions against alternatives; frame as *guessing with evidence*, not mind-reading.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** improves emotion *recognition* (impaired in this population) and, via contrast, sharpens the patient's own labels; reduces external-only focus by adding an inferential, mentalising step (EOT).
19. **Short-term outcome:** patient labels others' emotions with reasons and infers a need.
20. **Progression criteria:** demonstrates basic other-emotion reading → S7.

# SESSION 7 — Emotions and the Addiction Cycle (Integration with Using)

1. **Number:** 7
2. **Title:** How feelings connect to using — and to staying well.
3. **Main objective:** Explicitly link the emotional skills to the addiction/relapse cycle; replace substance-as-regulator with named coping (the "so what" session).
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) map emotion→craving→use for the patient's own pattern; (b) identify high-risk emotional triggers; (c) attach a specific alternative response to each; (d) reduce emotional/experiential avoidance.
5. **Skills:** emotion-craving linking, distress tolerance, self-soothing, adaptive coping/substitution, relapse-trigger planning. (Ladder Levels 6–8, 11–12.)
6. **Preparation:** the *craving & emotion diary*, *substance-use-as-coping*, *alternative coping*, and *relapse plan* worksheets; the patient's earlier sheets to show his own pattern.
7. **Duration:** 60 min.
8. **Check-in:** review other-reading HW.
9. **Psychoeducation (8 min):** "For many people, using is a way to switch off feelings they can't name or bear. Now that you can name them, you have other options. Feelings pass; you can ride them out." Introduce **emotion → craving → use** and that craving, like a wave, rises and falls.
10. **Activities:** (a) map his personal emotion→craving→use cycle; (b) name the emotions the drug was managing (often shame, hurt, boredom, loneliness, hopelessness, anger); (c) for the top 2–3 emotional triggers, choose a concrete alternative (name it, ground, express it, call someone, urge-surf, self-soothe).
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review HW; (2) draw his cycle; (3) identify emotions underneath the using; (4) build a trigger→alternative table; (5) practise one alternative (e.g., urge-surfing + self-soothe) in session; (6) HW: craving & emotion diary + one alternative; (7) ground + close.
12. **Language/questions:** "Just before you'd use, what feeling was usually there?" · "If the drug's job was to switch off that feeling, what else could do part of that job now?" · "Let's practise riding one wave without doing anything about it."
13. **Patient practice:** complete his cycle map; rehearse ≥1 alternative coping response.
14. **Worksheet:** *W9 Craving & emotion diary*, *W10 Substance use as emotional coping*, *W16 Alternative coping*, *W17 Relapse/emotional-trigger plan*, *W11 emotion under anger*.
15. **Homework:** craving & emotion diary; try one alternative when a trigger emotion shows up.
16. **Difficulties:** craving triggered *in session* by the topic; shame/hopelessness ("nothing works for me"); denial ("I only used for fun, not feelings"); overwhelm.
17. **Responding:** **treat in-session craving as a live practice opportunity** — ground, urge-surf, self-soothe, and debrief (see Module 5 for the craving protocol); meet shame with compassion and small wins; don't argue denial — stay curious ("were there ever times it was to feel *less* of something?"); slow down and stabilise if overwhelmed.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** consolidates DIF/DDF by using them on the highest-stakes material (emotions behind using), and targets the *functional core* of the self-medication model — substituting internal regulation for the drug.
19. **Short-term outcome:** patient articulates ≥1 emotion→craving link and one alternative response.
20. **Progression criteria:** can connect an emotion to craving and name an alternative → S8.

# SESSION 8 — Integration and Maintenance Planning

1. **Number:** 8
2. **Title:** Putting it together and keeping it going after discharge.
3. **Main objective:** Consolidate all skills into a personal, portable plan; anticipate post-discharge emotional high-risk situations; end well.
4. **Specific objectives:** (a) review gains across the Emotion Ladder; (b) build a one-page personal emotion-regulation + relapse plan; (c) reinforce self-efficacy; (d) manage ending.
5. **Skills:** integration, self-monitoring, emotional-regulation planning, relapse prevention, self-compassion. (Ladder Levels 10–12; transfer.)
6. **Preparation:** compile the patient's own worksheets; the *emotional regulation plan*, *weekly monitoring*, and *self-compassion* sheets.
7. **Duration:** 60 min.
8. **Check-in:** review craving/coping HW; acknowledge progress specifically.
9. **Psychoeducation (6 min):** "You've built a set of tools: notice → name → understand → express → manage. After discharge, feelings won't disappear — the plan is how you use these instead of the drug." Normalise setbacks and self-compassion.
10. **Activities:** (a) walk the Emotion Ladder and mark how far he's come; (b) co-write the one-page plan (top triggers, early warning feelings/body cues, go-to skills, support contacts, what to do after a slip); (c) a brief self-compassion exercise.
11. **Step-by-step:** (1) review + celebrate specific gains; (2) Ladder review; (3) build the one-page plan together; (4) self-compassion practice; (5) set up weekly self-monitoring; (6) address the ending/feelings about it; (7) ground + warm close.
12. **Language/questions:** "Which tool is *yours* now — the one you'll actually use?" · "When you notice [his early-warning feeling], what's the plan?" · "What would you say to a friend who slipped — can you say that to yourself?"
13. **Patient practice:** complete and read aloud his own plan.
14. **Worksheet:** *W15 Emotional regulation plan*, *W17 relapse plan*, *W19 self-compassion*, *W20 weekly emotional awareness monitoring*.
15. **Homework (post-discharge):** use weekly monitoring; carry the one-page plan.
16. **Difficulties:** anxiety about discharge/loss of support; "I'll be fine" minimisation; sadness at ending; hopelessness.
17. **Responding:** validate ending feelings (model the whole intervention); make the plan concrete and realistic; connect to aftercare/OST/follow-up per your setting; ensure any risk is handed to the treating team.
18. **Targets alexithymia:** consolidates and makes gains portable; the ending itself is a live opportunity to name and express feelings (transfer of DIF/DDF).
19. **Short-term outcome:** a completed personal plan the patient can articulate.
20. **Progression criteria:** end of protocol → **post-test (TAS-20, DERS, BDI-II)** per your Phase 4 timeline; refer onward as needed.

## 2.3 Adapting for opioid-dependent patients (your request section 5)

**Population realities to design around:** craving, withdrawal residue, emotional dysregulation, shame/guilt, anger, loneliness, interpersonal conflict, trauma, boredom, stress, hopelessness, frustration, avoidance/numbing, difficulty talking about feelings, low motivation, poor concentration, limited vocabulary, denial/minimisation, stigma, treatment fatigue.

**Standing adaptations:**
- **Short, concrete, repetitive.** ≤10-min teaching blocks; one idea; frequent recap; visuals.
- **Craving is content, not interruption.** When craving arises, use it as live material (name it, urge-surf, ground) — this *is* the intervention working.
- **Lead with shame-sensitivity.** Non-judgemental language; separate the person from the behaviour; expect and gently name shame as the emotion under much anger.
- **Boredom & treatment fatigue:** vary activities, use cards/movement, keep sessions active, acknowledge fatigue openly.
- **Denial/minimisation:** don't confront; stay curious and collaborative (MI-style engagement front-end), then return to skills.
- **Low motivation:** tie every skill to something *he* wants (less conflict at home, staying out, sleeping better).

**If the patient is…**
- **Minimally verbal:** use face cards, pointing, drawing on the body outline, ratings instead of talking; accept single words.
- **Resistant:** slow down, reduce demand, revisit rationale in his terms, find one relevant hook; never push.
- **Unable to identify emotions / "I don't know what I feel":** drop to the body (Session 2 skills) — start with sensation; offer 2–3 word choices; use faces; accept "restless."
- **Describes only physical symptoms:** validate the sensation, then *link* ("that knot in the stomach often goes with worry or dread — does either fit?").
- **Intellectualises everything (strong EOT):** gently interrupt the external narrative and redirect to body/here-and-now ("that's the story — what's happening *inside you* as you tell it?"); keep it short.
- **Becomes overwhelmed by emotion:** stop exploring, switch to grounding/self-soothing, down-titrate, re-stabilise, shorten; safety over depth.
- **Becomes angry:** don't defend; validate; look for the hurt/shame/fear underneath; ground; give space.
- **Starts craving in session:** run the craving protocol (Module 5) — normalise, urge-surf, ground, self-soothe, debrief.
- **Struggles with worksheets:** do them *with* him verbally; you write; use pictures/faces; make them optional-in-form but not in-content.
- **Limited literacy:** the whole low-literacy track — faces, colours, verbal, pictorial ratings (see each worksheet's low-literacy version in Module 4).

## 2.4 HIV-specific considerations (your request section 6)

Your design compares HIV+ and HIV− groups. HIV must be handled with skill and *without becoming the focus of every session* — the intervention target is alexithymia, not HIV counselling.

**How HIV-related experience can influence emotional life:**
- **Emotional awareness:** possible additional neurocognitive load (your synergistic-neurotoxicity framework) may make labelling harder — expect potentially slower gains in the HIV+ group (this is literally your moderation hypothesis).
- **Shame & stigma:** double stigma (drug use + HIV) intensifies concealment and the shame that suppresses emotion.
- **Fear/uncertainty:** health/future fears, disclosure fears.
- **Anger:** at diagnosis, at how it was acquired, at others.
- **Grief:** for health, identity, relationships, imagined future; possibly bereavement of others.
- **Interpersonal:** disclosure dilemmas, rejection, intimacy/relationship strain.
- **Adherence:** emotional states affecting ART/OST adherence; hopelessness → non-adherence.
- **Self-image & hopelessness:** "damaged," "no future" beliefs feeding both mood and using.

**How to address without making HIV the centre:**
- Let HIV-related emotions arise **as content when the patient raises them**, and process them with the *same skills* (name, locate, understand, express, regulate) as any other emotion — don't run a separate "HIV module."
- Keep confidentiality of HIV status absolute (your ethics requirement); never disclose status across contexts.
- If HIV-specific distress exceeds the scope of emotion-skills work (e.g., significant health anxiety, grief, adherence crisis, suicidality), **refer to the treating team / HIV counsellor** — that is a referral, not a session topic.

**Fidelity: keep IDENTICAL across both groups** (so between-group differences are interpretable):
- Number/order/titles of sessions, core objectives, worksheets, teaching content, therapist stance, dose (8×60 min), and the outcome-assessment procedure.

**May require clinical adaptation (document every deviation):**
- **Pacing:** HIV+ patients with fatigue/neurocognitive load may need slower pace, more repetition, shorter blocks *within the same session structure*.
- **Content that arises:** HIV-specific emotions get processed with the standard skills when raised.
- **Physical accommodations:** fatigue, medication timing, medical appointments.
- **Referral thresholds:** lower for HIV+ if medical/psychiatric complexity is higher.

> **Methodological caution:** any *systematic* difference in how you deliver to the two groups becomes a confound for your Group×Time interaction. Adapt *pace and support*, not *content and dose*, and log adaptations on the fidelity sheet (Module 5) so you can report them.
