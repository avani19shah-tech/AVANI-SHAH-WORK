## 5.1 Making the intervention measurable (your request section 11)

### 5.1.1 What should change if EFST works
If EFST reduces alexithymia, you should see **converging** changes:
- **Primary:** ↓ **TAS-20 total** (and a shift below cut-offs: ≥61 → 52–60 → ≤51). Use the **Reliable Change Index (RCI)** — you already list it — to classify who *individually* improved reliably, not just group means.
- **Subscales:** ↓ **DIF** and ↓ **DDF** first and most; **EOT** may move little or last (state this a priori — it protects you from over-interpreting a null EOT effect).
- **Secondary/corroborating:** ↓ **DERS** (esp. Clarity and Awareness subscales, which conceptually overlap with DIF/DDF), independent of **BDI-II** change (you use BDI-II as covariate to separate mood from alexithymia).
- **Behavioural/in-session:** more feeling words, faster naming, more differentiation, less "I don't know," more spontaneous inner-focus.

### 5.1.2 Which changes reflect DIF / DDF / EOT
- **DIF improvement:** can distinguish emotions from bodily sensations; picks a specific emotion where before there was "bad/nothing"; can rate intensity; links body cues to feelings. (TAS items on confusion about feelings, bodily sensations.)
- **DDF improvement:** produces feeling words to another person; completes "I feel… because…"; explains a feeling rather than "can't explain."
- **EOT improvement (expect least):** shows curiosity about inner causes; talks about feelings when relevant rather than only external facts; endorses that examining feelings is useful. **Note:** EOT is the most trait-like and least responsive component and has known psychometric weaknesses (its items are more affected by education/verbal ability and cross-cultural factors). Interpret EOT change cautiously.

### 5.1.3 Behavioural indicators to monitor in session
Rate each session (0–3 anchors below): (a) number of distinct feeling words used; (b) latency/ease of naming a feeling; (c) differentiation (global vs specific); (d) body–emotion linking; (e) spontaneous inner focus vs external-only; (f) "I don't know" frequency; (g) expression to another (role-play); (h) use of a coping/grounding skill.

### 5.1.4 Qualitative indicators to document
Verbatim shifts ("bad" → "hurt and ashamed"); first spontaneous emotion label; first "I feel… because…"; first time he grounds instead of escalating; first emotion→craving link he makes himself. Keep a short **process note** per session (see fidelity sheet).

### 5.1.5 Session-by-session progress indicators
Tie to the Emotion Ladder: S1–2 body/basic naming; S3 vocabulary range; S4 trigger→feeling chains; S5 voiced feeling-statements; S6 other-emotion reading; S7 emotion→craving link + alternative; S8 articulated personal plan.

### 5.1.6 Distinguishing genuine improvement from socially-desirable answering (your key validity worry)
This is the **self-report paradox** you already noted in your limitations — take it seriously.
- **Triangulate:** require self-report (TAS-20/W20) to be **corroborated by observed in-session behaviour** and by **DERS** (a different self-report construct) and, where you use them, **performance/projective indices (Rorschach affect variables, TAT SCORS-G)** which are less fakeable. Convergence across methods = more credible.
- **Watch for a paradoxical TAS-20 pattern:** some patients, as awareness improves, *notice* more deficits and may not drop (or briefly rise) on DIF — a known measurement artefact. Frame this a priori.
- **Behavioural anchors** (5.1.3) are harder to fake than questionnaire items.
- **Blind scoring** of projective/behavioural measures by a rater unaware of time-point/group reduces bias (your single-therapist design otherwise risks demand effects — see 5.4).
- **Consistency checks:** genuine change shows up across multiple sessions and multiple indicators, not as a single post-test jump.

### 5.1.7 Do not invent instruments
Use only established, cited measures. Verified anchors:
- **TAS-20** — Bagby, Parker & Taylor (1994), *J Psychosomatic Research* 38(1):23–32; three factors DIF/DDF/EOT; cut-offs ≤51 / 52–60 / ≥61. **Use the validated Hindi adaptation** and cite whichever Hindi validation you actually use (verify it yourself before citing — do not cite a Hindi validation you haven't read).
- **DERS** — Gratz & Roemer (2004), *J Psychopathology and Behavioral Assessment* 26(1):41–54.
- **BDI-II** — Beck, Steer & Brown (1996).
- **SDS** — Gossop et al. (1995), *Addiction* 90(5):607–614.
- For a *performance-based* alexithymia complement (optional, strengthens the self-report paradox response), consider the **Level of Emotional Awareness Scale (LEAS)** — Lane et al. (1990) — but only if you can administer/score it properly.

## 5.7 Potential confounding variables (your request 16G)
Grouped so you can address each in design/analysis:
- **State/clinical:** stage of abstinence/withdrawal severity, sleep, pain, current depression/anxiety (→ BDI-II covariate), dependence severity (→ SDS covariate), craving level.
- **Medication:** OST (buprenorphine/methadone) dose/timing, ART regimen and side-effects, psychotropics — all may affect affect/cognition.
- **HIV-specific:** CD4/viral load, HIV duration, neurocognitive status, ART adherence, time since diagnosis, stigma exposure.
- **Demographic/ability:** age, education, literacy, verbal ability (affect EOT especially), native language vs test language.
- **Contextual:** concurrent standard de-addiction programme (a major confound — improvements may be milieu, not EFST), family visits/conflict, ward events, length of stay.
- **Measurement:** self-report bias / social desirability, alexithymia self-report paradox, practice/retest effects (esp. Rorschach/TAT within 4 weeks), therapist demand effects (single therapist).
- **Trait vs state:** some baseline "alexithymia" is state post-detox and will improve *without* EFST (regression toward the mean / natural recovery).

## 5.8 Methodological problems & how to reduce them (your request 16H)
| Problem | Reduction strategy |
|---|---|
| No untreated control (can't attribute change to EFST) | Frame as pilot; use RCI for individual change; measure a non-targeted construct as a quasi-control; be explicit in discussion; recommend future RCT |
| Natural recovery / milieu confound | Both groups get same standard care; report standard-care content; consider a brief pre-baseline stability check |
| Self-report paradox / social desirability | Triangulate self-report + behavioural anchors + DERS + blind-scored projective/performance measures |
| Single therapist / demand effects | Blind, independent scoring of outcomes; manualise + fidelity checklist; audio-review a sample for adherence (with consent) |
| Practice effects on Rorschach/TAT | Note as limitation; blind scoring; interpret projective change cautiously |
| Small N / underpowered interaction | Report effect sizes + CIs, not just p; treat interaction as exploratory; power discussion |
| State vs trait confound | ≥7-day post-detox rule (you have it); covary BDI-II/SDS; consider assessing withdrawal severity |
| Group non-equivalence (HIV+ vs −) | Compare baseline covariates; ANCOVA; report differences honestly |
| Missing data / dropout (treatment fatigue) | Track attendance; plan for attrition; report completer vs intention-to-treat |
| Fidelity drift / unequal delivery across groups | Fidelity checklist every session; log all adaptations; keep content/dose identical |

## 5.9 CLINICAL SAFETY BOUNDARIES (your request section 15)

This is not optional. Emotional processing is **not** appropriate at every moment, and a brief skills course is **stabilisation-first**.

**Slow down / do NOT deepen emotional exploration when:**
- The patient is in significant withdrawal, acutely intoxicated, medically unstable, sleep-deprived, or severely depressed.
- Arousal is climbing beyond a tolerable window (visible distress, shaking, dissociation, escalating anger, urge to flee).
- Trauma material surfaces that cannot be safely contained in the remaining time.
- Early in the protocol (Sessions 1–3) — build capacity before depth.

**Managing overwhelming affect (in order):**
1. Stop the exploration; name what's happening ("this got big — let's slow down together").
2. **Ground:** feet on floor, longer exhale, orient to the room (name 5 things seen), cool water.
3. Down-shift to a concrete, external, calming focus.
4. Do not end on peak arousal — stay until settled; shorten the session if needed.
5. Debrief briefly and log it; inform the treating team if significant.

**When to use grounding:** any rising distress/craving, dissociation, trauma activation, or before ending a charged session. Teach it early (Session 2) so it's available.

**When to return to stabilisation (step back down the ladder):** repeated flooding, worsening sleep/mood, increased craving after sessions, dissociation, or the patient dreading sessions. Prioritise regulation skills (Levels 11) over depth (Levels 8–10).

**Acute craving during a session (protocol):**
1. Normalise ("cravings are expected; this is a chance to practise").
2. Rate it 0–10.
3. **Urge-surf:** describe it as a wave that rises and falls; breathe through it.
4. Ground + self-soothe.
5. Re-rate (usually falls) → concretise the learning ("it passed without using").
6. If craving is severe/persistent or risk emerges, involve the treating team.

**When psychiatric/medical referral is necessary (refer to the treating team — do NOT manage alone):**
- Suicidal or homicidal ideation, intent, or plan (follow your institution's risk protocol immediately).
- Psychosis, severe depression, mania, or marked deterioration.
- Severe/complex trauma reactions beyond skills-level containment.
- Medical instability, severe withdrawal, overdose risk, uncontrolled pain.
- HIV-specific medical/psychiatric crises, adherence crises, or grief beyond scope.

**Contraindications / situations requiring modification:** active psychosis (excluded in your criteria), significant cognitive impairment/HAD (excluded), acute intoxication/withdrawal (wait), high acute suicidality (stabilise/refer first), unmanaged severe PTSD (trauma-focused care first; keep EFST light and stabilising).

**Avoid inadvertently triggering trauma-related distress:**
- Don't push interoceptive/eyes-closed work with trauma-loaded patients; keep eyes open, brief, choiceful.
- Ask permission before deepening; give explicit control ("we can stop any time").
- Title-rate: little bits of affect + lots of grounding.
- Screen for trauma history from the chart; flag to supervision; refer for trauma-specific treatment when indicated — an 8-session emotion-skills course is **not** trauma therapy.

**Hard boundaries (do not cross):**
- No unsupervised detoxification advice, no medication initiation/changes/tapering advice — that is the treating psychiatrist's role.
- Nothing here overrides your institution's risk, ethics, and supervision protocols. When in doubt, stabilise, keep safe, and escalate.
